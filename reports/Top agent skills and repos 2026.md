# Workflow discipline beats star counts in 2026

As of 17 October 2026, two items dominate the agent-skill ecosystem by measured installs: Anthropic's **frontend-design** plugin, with **1,134,112 installs**, and Jesse Vincent's **Superpowers** workflow pack, with **1,009,371 installs**. Both figures come from the official Claude plugin directory. Superpowers also leads community packs on GitHub with **301k stars**. The next tier mixes Anthropic's own quality plugins (code-review, code-simplifier, CLAUDE.md management, security-guidance), three MCP servers distributed as plugins (Context7, Playwright, GitHub), and Matt Pocock's `mattpocock/skills`. Pocock's pack spreads mainly through Vercel's cross-agent `npx skills` channel rather than Anthropic's directory. In the wider repo world, the most-starred projects are open, agent-agnostic harnesses (OpenClaw 392k, OpenCode 215k), GitHub's Spec Kit (142k) and a 2026 wave of agent-fleet managers (Paperclip, Orca, herdr). What engineers actually praise is narrower: skills that force the agent to **clarify, plan, test and review before it writes code**, live-docs and browser MCPs that close feedback loops, and admin-controlled distribution for businesses. The criticisms are consistent. Every installed skill costs context on every turn. Auto-triggering is unreliable once you exceed the description budget. Mega-packs churn. And open skill registries contain malicious skills at rates of roughly 1 in 300 to nearly 1 in 2, depending on the registry. For an expert Claude Code or Antigravity user, the right move is a small, curated stack: one workflow pack, a few first-party quality plugins, two or three MCP servers, and a deliberately empty "skip" list for viral mega-repos. One caution on the evidence: the GitHub and plugin-directory numbers below were pulled live on 2026-10-17, but almost all practitioner praise and criticism comes from search-result snippets, not full pages. So the numbers are firm and the sentiment is directional.

## Two distribution channels crown different winners

The single most important thing to understand about 2026 skill popularity is that **there are two leaderboards, and they disagree**. The first is Anthropic's official directory at claude.com/plugins, installed with `/plugin install`. It listed **341 plugins** on 17 October 2026, up from 276 on 31 July and 291 on 8 September ([claude.com/plugins](https://claude.com/plugins); [vibecoding.app](https://vibecoding.app/blog/best-claude-code-plugins)). The second is Vercel's skills.sh registry and its `npx skills` CLI. That channel launched on 20 January 2026 and installs skills into any compatible agent ([ToolWorthy](https://www.toolworthy.ai/tool/skills-sh)). Matt Pocock's pack shows how far apart the two can be. Third-party trackers put its flagship `grill-me` skill at roughly **508k installs** ([skillselion guide](https://skillselion.com/guide/matt-pocock-skills-map)), yet its official Claude directory listing shows just **1,745 installs** ([claude.com](https://claude.com/plugins/mattpocock-skills)). Neither number is wrong. They measure different audiences.

The official directory's install counts are the most reliable usage signal anywhere in this ecosystem. They are first-party, they count installs rather than bookmarks, and they were fetched directly on the research date.

Three patterns stand out. First, **workflow and quality tooling beats knowledge**. Eight of the top eleven change *how* Claude works (review, simplification, memory hygiene, security hooks, structured phases) rather than teaching it a domain. Second, **"plugin" now means MCP distribution too**. Context7, Playwright and GitHub, all MCP servers, sit in the top seven. Third, Superpowers is the only community pack in the top tier, at about 89% of frontend-design's installs. Anthropic's famous document skills (docx, pptx, xlsx, pdf) barely register here. They ship built into Claude.ai and Cowork rather than as directory installs, which is why `anthropics/skills` has **181,779 stars** but no comparable install count ([GitHub](https://github.com/anthropics/skills)).

On skills.sh, a July 2026 snapshot (search snippet only, because the site could not be fetched) found **three authors holding the entire top 10**: Vercel, Pocock and Anthropic. The top four were `find-skills` (Vercel, ~2.72M), `frontend-design` (~716k), `grill-me` (~691k) and `agent-browser` (Vercel, ~591k) ([dev.to/Skillselion](https://dev.to/skillselion/the-entire-claude-code-skills-top-10-belongs-to-three-authors-anthropic-holds-one-slot-27jc)). Discount `find-skills`. It is a meta-skill that installs other skills, and it is likely surfaced by the CLI itself, so its #1 spot reflects distribution mechanics more than engineer preference. The author of that leaderboard analysis also runs a competing skill directory. The scale of growth is still real: the top skill had only about 26,000 installs in January 2026 ([ToolWorthy](https://www.toolworthy.ai/tool/skills-sh)).

The praise clusters tightly around **requirement interrogation and enforced discipline**. Superpowers' brainstorming is described as a Socratic exchange. The framework "refuses to write code" until it has asked clarifying questions and proposed a design ([Marc Nuri](https://blog.marcnuri.com/superpowers-claude-code-skills-framework); [DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/tutorial/claude-code-superpowers)). A "Rave Review of Superpowers" on Hacker News credited its adversarial subagent review of its own spec and plan with catching "several things I would've missed" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47623101)). Pocock's `grill-me` makes the same move from a different angle: the user gets "relentlessly interviewed about a plan or design until every branch of the decision tree is resolved" ([mcp.directory](https://mcp.directory/blog/matt-pocock-skills-the-npm-moment-explained-2026)). Every's compound-engineering adds a distinctive final step that writes reusable learnings into the repo. It was born when Kieran Klaassen noticed Claude citing its own earlier PR reviews and applying "three months of review taste" unprompted ([developertoolkit.ai](https://developertoolkit.ai/en/method/principles/compound-engineering/)). Simon Willison's format-level praise explains why this works at all. Skill metadata costs "a few dozen extra tokens" and the full body loads only when relevant, which led him to call skills possibly "a bigger deal than MCP" ([beri.net summary](https://www.beri.net/learning/simonwillison-claude-skills-bigger-than-mcp)).

The criticisms are just as specific. On Superpowers' TDD, one HN commenter argued it is "not really TDD": it writes acceptance tests and then tweaks output until they pass ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47623101)). The pack also replaced independent subagent review loops with inline self-review to cut roughly 25-minute runs, trading rigour for speed ([Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/claude-code-superpowers-plugin-guide)). Critics note that "nothing in Superpowers requires the plugin specifically", since every skill is plain markdown you could write yourself ([Marc Nuri](https://blog.marcnuri.com/superpowers-claude-code-skills-framework)). Compound-engineering is called "churny" and "opinionated": it ships near-daily releases and renamed every command from `/workflows:*` to `/ce-*` ([rywalker.com](https://rywalker.com/research/compound-engineering-plugin)). ECC, which grew out of an Anthropic hackathon win and rose from roughly 82k to 282k stars in 2026, is described as "dividing the developer community" ([Medium](https://medium.com/@tentenco/everything-claude-code-inside-the-82k-star-agent-harness-thats-dividing-the-developer-community-4fe54feccbc1)).

The deepest structural critique applies to every pack. **Each installed skill injects its name and description into context on every turn, used or not** ([dev.to/haoli](https://dev.to/haoli/your-claude-code-skills-are-being-silently-demoted-i-built-a-cli-to-catch-it-454e)). Past a default budget of about **15,000 characters**, Claude Code silently drops descriptions, so skills stop auto-triggering. Jesse Vincent documented this himself, and the budget can be raised with `SLASH_COMMAND_TOOL_CHAR_BUDGET` ([blog.fsck.com](https://blog.fsck.com/2025/12/17/claude-code-skills-not-triggering/)). One developer reports only about **50% autonomous triggering** even after fixing the budget. That is a single post, so treat it as anecdotal ([dev.to](https://dev.to/lizechengnet/why-claude-code-skills-dont-trigger-and-how-to-fix-them-in-2026-o7h)). Anthropic's own API skill once consumed over **200k tokens** before v2.1.234 cut it to about 25k ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/claude-code-token-reduction/)). This is why 380-skill and 2,600-skill catalogs such as `alirezarezvani/claude-skills` and `sickn33/agentic-awesome-skills` are reference libraries, not installs. It also explains why a token-saving novelty like caveman reached 113k stars: it is a symptom of context pressure, not a cure for it. No independent benchmark measures whether any workflow pack improves outcomes. The evidence for Superpowers, Pocock and compound-engineering is entirely practitioner testimony and revealed preference.

**Harnesses.** OpenClaw is the year's attention phenomenon, at roughly 1,200 stars per day over its life ([GitHub](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw)). It is also the clearest cautionary tale. CertiK warned in March 2026 of unauthorized actions and crypto theft, citing about **280 security advisories and ~100 CVEs** ([Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/openclaw-exposes-users-to-crypto-theft-aad10137)), and the August "2.0" release was framed as a security response ([Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/what-shipped-in-openclaw-2-0/)). OpenCode's appeal is open source plus roughly 75 model providers. Even in February, though, when it already led Claude Code on stars (112k to 71k), comparisons credited Claude Code with leading on actual usage ([MorphLLM](https://www.morphllm.com/comparisons/opencode-vs-claude-code)). Reports that Anthropic blocked OpenCode from using Claude Pro/Max OAuth around January 2026 are weakly sourced, and I found no Anthropic statement ([Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/anthropic-blocks-opencode-identity-in-claude-3ca723fe)). Those reports still help explain why engineers favour harness-agnostic tooling.

**Orchestrators.** A second generation of 2026-born fleet managers, **Paperclip (104k), Orca (95k) and herdr (45k)**, has overtaken 2025's worktree tools: vibe-kanban (28k), Claude Squad (8.6k) and Crystal (3k, now renamed Nimbalyst). Their very large open-issue counts (Paperclip 6,092, Orca 8,540) signal heavy real use and immaturity at the same time ([Paperclip](https://github.com/paperclipai/paperclip); [Orca](https://github.com/stablyai/orca); [herdr](https://github.com/herdrdev/herdr)). Steve Yegge's Gas Town has mindshare far beyond its 18k stars. Maggie Appleton argues it is worth taking seriously as a signal of where development is heading, with durable work records (Beads) coordinating a colony of role-based workers. She also calls it "entirely vibecoded" and budget-burning, not a working tool for today's developers ([Maggie Appleton](https://maggieappleton.com/gastown)). Benn Stancil says it "uses AI in the most aggressive, expensive, and explosive ways possible" ([Benn Stancil](https://benn.substack.com/p/gas-town)). The Ralph loop went from Geoffrey Huntley's "Ralph is a bash loop" to an official Anthropic Stop-hook plugin with `--completion-promise` and `--max-iterations`. The caveats are API cost and sandboxing ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-ralph-wiggum-went-from-the-simpsons-to-the-biggest-name-in-ai-right-now)).

**Spec-driven development** became a top-tier category, led by Spec Kit, OpenSpec, the now-archived GSD, and BMAD. The sharpest critique comes from Thoughtworks' Birgitta Böckeler. She found that almost every SDD tool is "spec-first" only: Spec Kit's specs live for a single change request, not a feature. She also warns that the more ambitious "spec-as-source" approach risks "the downsides of both MDD and LLMs: inflexibility and non-determinism" ([Martin Fowler / Böckeler](https://www.martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html)). GSD's archiving and the absence of usage data suggest the stars overstate durable adoption. For an expert who already runs a plan-review loop, Spec Kit's ceremony mostly duplicates what Superpowers or `grill-me` plus a `to-spec` skill already provide.

**Context tooling moved past docs lookups.** Context7 (63k stars, 418k plugin installs) remains the default docs MCP. A wave of 2026 code-graph and token-compression repos exploded alongside it: Graphify 128k, codegraph 74k, codebase-memory-mcp 47k, rtk 84k, headroom 76k ([graphify](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify); [codegraph](https://github.com/colbymchenry/codegraph); [rtk](https://github.com/rtk-ai/rtk); [headroom](https://github.com/headroomlabs-ai/headroom)). I found no independent practitioner reviews of these. Their star velocity reflects the same context-cost anxiety that made caveman viral, so treat them as experiments. Serena (30k) is the more established option: an LSP-backed semantic retrieval and editing MCP ([Serena](https://github.com/oraios/serena)).

## Businesses buy governed bundles, not individual skills

Enterprises praise the **delivery mechanism** more than any single skill. Anthropic's December 2025 launch let Team and Enterprise admins provision skills org-wide, on by default and toggleable per user. The partner directory included Notion, Canva, Figma and Atlassian, with Vercel co-building a deploy skill ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/organization-skills-and-directory)). Cowork plugins (30 January 2026) bundle skills, connectors, slash commands and subagents. The 24 February update added private marketplaces, admin auto-install, OpenTelemetry cost monitoring, Google Workspace, DocuSign and WordPress connectors, and partner plugins from Slack, LSEG, S&P Global and others ([WinBuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/02/25/anthropic-claude-cowork-13-enterprise-plugins-google-workspace-docusign-xcxwbn/)). Admins can now restrict individual MCP tool actions, for example letting a Gmail connector read but not send ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/anthropic-takes-claude-cowork-out-of-preview-and-straight-into-the-enterprise/)).

The most visible business plugins are finance and legal. Claude for Financial Services added an Excel add-in and six finance skills (comps, DCF, due diligence, teasers, earnings, initiating coverage). Norway's NBIM reports about a **20% productivity gain, roughly 213,000 hours** ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/advancing-claude-for-financial-services)). The legal plugin's contract and NDA triage coincided with Thomson Reuters falling about **18% in a day**, though analysts called the reaction overdone and Anthropic says the plugin needs attorney review ([Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/software-stocks-plunge-285b-as-anthropics-claude-enters-legal-automation/)). Usage data shows that, by sessions, these tools serve mostly non-engineers. Across 1.2M Cowork sessions, business operations accounted for 33.4% and software development only 8.7% ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/anthropic-says-customers-are-using-claude-cowork-for-the-work-around-work-as-ai-companies-soften-job-loss-rhetoric-160000661.html)). Engineering case studies carry the hardest numbers, all vendor-published: Rakuten cut time to market 79% (24 to 5 days) ([Claude customers](https://claude.com/customers/rakuten)), Ramp reports 1M+ lines in 30 days and 80% faster incident response ([Claude customers](https://claude.com/customers/ramp)), and PwC claims delivery up to 70% faster ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/pwc-expanded-partnership)). The same companies also run Codex ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/04/openai-codex-growth-enterprise-ai-agents/)).

That multi-vendor reality is why the SKILL.md format won. OpenAI's Codex docs state that its skills "build on the open agent skills standard" ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/codex/skills)). Google's October 2026 unified Gemini agent "uses skills and tools" ([Constellation Research](https://constellationr.com/insights/news/google-cloud-launches-gemini-agent-work-across-enterprise-systems)). Antigravity reads SKILL.md from `.agents/skills/` ([Google Codelab](https://codelabs.developers.google.com/getting-started-with-antigravity-skills?hl=en)). For a dual Claude Code / Antigravity user, a skill written once is now genuinely portable. That makes cross-agent packs like Pocock's, installed via `npx skills`, more valuable than Claude-only plugins wherever both would work.

## Four caveats that should change how you read every table

**Stars are not usage.** Pocock's ~295k stars against 1,745 directory installs, and OpenCode out-starring Claude Code while trailing it on usage, both show stars measuring attention from a cross-agent audience that includes Codex, Cursor and OpenClaw users. Many of 2026's most-starred repos are single-purpose viral skills (caveman, humanizer, archify) or repos with no independent practitioner coverage at all. Examples are `morluto/rea` (306k stars in six months) and `ponytail` (166k in four months) ([GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=claude-code+created%3A%3E2025-12-31+stars%3A%3E5000&type=repositories&s=stars)). The official directory's install counts are the best usage proxy available, and even they measure installs, not retained use.

**Fake stars are documented in exactly this category.** A CMU study accepted at ICSE 2026 found about **6M suspected fake stars across 18,617 repos**. AI/LLM repos were the largest non-malicious category, with ~177k fake stars, at prices of $0.03–$0.85 per star ([arXiv 2412.13459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13459)). Every repo in this report passes the fork-ratio heuristic, but that heuristic is weak, and nobody has audited these specific repos.

**Much of the sentiment rests on snippets.** Hacker News, Reddit, skills.sh and dev.to pages could not be fetched. The Superpowers praise and criticism, the trigger-reliability reports, the skills.sh leaderboard, and most of the Gas Town and OpenClaw commentary therefore come from search-result summaries that may paraphrase. Business ROI figures are vendor-published or come from consultancies that resell the capability. Headcount claims for Deloitte (~470k) and Cognizant (~350k) appear only in trade blogs. I found no criticism of frontend-design specifically, which may reflect a gap in what could be fetched rather than an absence of complaints.

**Malicious skills are common in open registries.** ESET analysed about 900,000 skills and found more than **3,000 directly malicious**, about 1 in 300 ([Expert Insights](https://expertinsights.com/news/1-in-300-ai-agent-skills-is-directly-malicious-eset-research-finds)). Snyk found critical issues in 13.4% of ~4,000 skills, with 76 confirmed malicious payloads ([Forkast](https://forkast.news/ai-agent-skills-are-the-new-supply-chain-attack-vector-and-most-have-never-been-vetted)). An academic study of 238,180 skills reports malicious shares of **46.8% on ClawHub and 23.0% on skills.sh** ([arXiv 2603.16572](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.16572v1)). The MalSkills scan confirmed 400 malicious skills across seven registries ([arXiv 2603.27204](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27204)). Scanners are not a safety net. Trail of Bits reportedly bypassed the ClawHub, Cisco and skills.sh scanners. In another test, a benign "fake" skill promoted with an Instagram ad reached about 26,000 agents, including corporate accounts, and passed every scanner ([Aviatrix](https://aviatrix.ai/threat-research-center/the-sorry-state-of-skill-distribution-2026)). The Cloud Security Alliance also documented a "FakeGit" campaign of bait repos impersonating skills and MCP servers ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/CSA_research_note_fakegit-agentbaiting-mcp-supply-chain_20260721-csa-styled.pdf)). The practical rule follows directly: read every SKILL.md and any bundled scripts before installing, prefer Anthropic-verified or first-party publishers, pin to a commit, and never let an agent install skills on its own.

## What to install first

The recommendation below assumes an expert who already runs a lean CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md, verification gates and reviewer subagents. It prioritises tools with **measured installs, first-party maintenance and a closed feedback loop**, and it keeps the total skill-description footprint well under the ~15,000-character trigger budget.

| Order | Install | Claude Code | Antigravity | Why this, and the trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One** workflow pack: Superpowers *or* mattpocock/skills | `/plugin install` Superpowers, or `npx skills add mattpocock/skills` | Pocock via `npx skills` into `.agents/skills/` | The best-evidenced "process" win. Pick one: overlap wastes budget and causes conflicting triggers. Superpowers is more prescriptive; Pocock is composable and cross-agent |
| 2 | Context7 | Directory plugin | `agy mcp add` | 418k installs. Removes the most common failure (stale APIs) |
| 3 | Code Review + Security Guidance | Directory plugins (Anthropic) | Port the reviewer as a skill | First-party; the confidence threshold ≥80 keeps noise down; security hook is passive |
| 4 | Browser loop: Chrome DevTools MCP *or* Playwright MCP | Pick one | Usually skip: Antigravity has a built-in browser agent | DevTools for debugging and perf, Playwright for E2E suites |
| 5 | GitHub MCP (or just `gh` CLI) | Directory plugin | `agy mcp add` | Official; skip it if `gh` already covers your use and you want fewer tool schemas |
| 6 | frontend-design (only if you ship UI) | Directory plugin | Copy the SKILL.md | Most-installed item anywhere; choose it *or* ui-ux-pro-max |
| 7 | codex-plugin-cc | Plugin | n/a (use Antigravity's Gemini as the second model) | Cheap cross-model second opinion on diffs |
| 8 | CLAUDE.md Management + Code Simplifier | Directory plugins | Equivalent skill | Keeps memory lean; post-change cleanup |

The next tier is worth evaluating against a specific need, not installing by default. **Serena** helps on large, statically typed codebases where grep-driven navigation wastes tokens. **Ralph Loop** suits bounded, test-gated grinds with `--max-iterations` set. **Compound-engineering's "compound" step** can be borrowed by hand to capture learnings into docs. **Beads** gives durable, git-backed task memory across sessions. **claude-mem** is an option only if native memory falls short; it injects compressed history into every session, so measure the context cost. **Spec Kit or OpenSpec** fit teams that need spec artifacts for humans, not as a replacement for a plan-review loop. **claude-hud** makes context consumption visible. For parallelism, Orca or herdr are the 2026 leaders, but their issue counts argue for waiting unless native subagents and worktrees are genuinely the bottleneck.

The skip list matters as much. Don't install mega-packs wholesale (ECC, 380- and 2,600-skill catalogs, wshobson's full marketplace), because they blow the description budget and import other people's opinions. Read them and copy individual skills. Skip caveman and other unreviewed token-compression proxies until someone publishes a quality-versus-savings measurement. Keep OpenClaw, ruflo and Gas Town out of production machines: the first for its CVE record, the latter two because their own advocates describe them as expensive experiments. Leave archived projects alone (GSD, Roo Code). Never install anything from ClawHub or from an unvetted skills.sh listing without reading it.

## Conclusion

The 2026 evidence inverts the usual reading of GitHub popularity. The repos with the most stars (OpenClaw, ECC, viral one-skill repos) are mostly *attention*. The tools with the most measured installs are almost boringly specific: a design skill, a methodology that refuses to code until it understands the task, a five-agent reviewer, a docs server and a browser driver. What the winners share is that each one either **forces a missing engineering step** (clarify, review, test, simplify) or **injects ground truth** (docs, browser state, repo state). Neither depends on which harness you run. That is why the SKILL.md format spread to Codex, Antigravity and Gemini within months, and why the expert's advantage now lies in curation rather than accumulation.

The next constraint is trust, not capability. Context budgets cap how many skills help before they hurt, and malicious-skill rates in open registries run from about 1 in 300 to nearly half. The skill ecosystem is therefore heading where npm went: curated, verified, admin-provisioned channels for businesses, and pinned, hand-read installs for individuals. The practical bet for the next six months is a small personal skill set you own and version, ported across Claude Code and Antigravity, drawn from two or three trusted upstreams and audited like any other dependency.
