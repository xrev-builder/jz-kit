## Modeling takeaways (for the fantasy model)

1. **ACL by position is the clearest gradient:** QB ~92% RTP with ~0 production loss; RB ~65% RTP with modest efficiency loss (YPC ~-0.3) but only ~20% return to ≥85% of prior fantasy output in Y1; WR ~60–69% RTP with the largest and most persistent production loss. A "year-2 bounce" is not supported for RBs (only 4/36 improved Y1→Y2); age <26 is the main moderator. Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33553449/ ; https://injurypronenewsletter.beehiiv.com/p/welcome-back-second-year-acl-myth ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666061X20301723
2. **Achilles is worse than ACL for skill players:** 27% recover to prior level; performance decline ~21% beyond aging, worst in Y1; WR/RB RTP lowest (38% WR), TE highest (71%). Model TEs (Kittle) more favorably than RB/WR but still apply a Y1 haircut. Sources: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00913847.2026.2682120 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34609159/
3. **Hamstring:** same-season re-injury ~12–15%, ~38% recur in a later season; risk concentrated in the first 2 weeks back and highest for WRs; performance dip is short-lived. Treat any WR with ≥2 hamstring strains in 4 years (e.g., Tyson) as a chronic risk. Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34878369/ ; https://www.foxsports.com/stories/nfl/nfl-injuries-why-hamstring-ailments-are-common-for-skill-position-players
4. **High-ankle sprain:** mean ~2–7 weeks lost depending on grade; low true recurrence but next-season fantasy production is significantly depressed for RB/WR/TE (303-player study), and early returners (≤3 games) rarely regain form quickly. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10999692/ ; https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/high-ankle-sprains-their-effect-on-fantasy-performance-fantasy-football/
5. **Back/disc:** on-field stats are generally preserved after discectomy (74–82% RTP) but careers shorten and contracts shrink; QBs fare best. Treat as availability/longevity risk more than per-game production risk. Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20818192/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13101341/
6. **Lisfranc / foot:** RTP high (82–90%+) but ~11 months and a measurable AV drop in Y1; Jones fractures recover fully unless rushed (<10 wks). Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10087650/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10819528/
7. **Concussion:** ~1.5 games missed; per-game production roughly intact for RB/WR, but elevated same-season MSK injury risk (+83% upper-body) and higher release rates. Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31386551/ ; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02699052.2026.2680662
8. **Knee scope/meniscus:** 2–6 weeks for partial meniscectomy, 80–86% RTP; long-run cartilage cost matters more for young players (Rice's 2026 cleanup). Sources: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2059775421002807 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29112467/
9. **Turf toe:** non-op returns in ~11–14 weeks with normal performance; surgical (Grade 3, e.g., Burrow 2025) ~7 months with worse post-op performance in the cohort. Source: https://orthojournalhms.org/22/2021article20_25.html ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32577105/
10. **QB shoulder:** AC sprains are almost always non-operative (5–6 weeks); labral repairs return ~87% but throwing-arm labrum surgery carries a real performance-decline risk. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5825334/ ; https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23259671261454331
11. **Generic next-season penalty:** any time-loss injury costs about -0.5 PPG next season on average (QB -1.95, WR -0.33, RB -0.7) per the Cureus/PMC fantasy-points study — a reasonable default prior when injury type is unknown. Source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12804431/
