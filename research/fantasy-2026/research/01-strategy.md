# 01 — Winning Draft Strategy: Evidence Review (2018–2025 seasons)

**Prepared:** 2026-09-03 (one week before 2026 Week 1)
**League:** ESPN, 10 teams, full PPR, QB / 2 RB / 2 WR / TE / 2 FLEX (RB-WR-TE) / DST, no K, 6 bench + 1 IR, 15-round snake.
**Slots:** Pick 2 (4-pt pass TD league) and Pick 4 (6-pt pass TD league). Objective: championship equity, variance accepted.

## Method and limitations (read first)

- Every quantitative claim below is tied to a URL in the Sources list. Numbers were captured from search-engine excerpts of those pages; the research sandbox's egress proxy blocked full-page fetches of every fantasy site tried (PFF, ETR, Underdog Network, FantasyPros, RotoViz, 4for4, FTN, Footballguys, RotoWire, Yahoo, ESPN, CBS, NBC, substacks). Figures should therefore be treated as **accurately attributed but not re-read at the source**; anything I could not corroborate from at least one excerpt is marked **UNVERIFIED**.
- Where a study's sample period is wider than 2018–2025 (e.g., 2015–2025), it is noted. Best-ball (Underdog BBM) data is used as the largest available sample of "what wins"; it is 12-team, half-PPR, 1 FLEX, no waivers, so directional lessons transfer but exact rates do not.
- Because full pages were not readable, no 2026-specific projections/ADP beyond a handful of top-20 names are included; the companion player-level research should cover that.

**Reading:** Round-1 hit rates at RB and WR are both only ~50–55% for a top-12 finish, and the bust rate is ~1 in 3. WRs have been marginally safer in Round 1; the positions converge afterward. For a 10-team home league that needs one title, the tail matters more.

**Reading:** Across 2020–2025, the most robust construction is **one elite RB + three or four WRs in the first six rounds, then a cluster of 3–4 RBs in rounds 7–11**. The RB share of "league winners" (39%) is higher than WR (30%) because RB outcomes are more concentrated — which argues for owning one true RB1 and then buying RB *volume* late, not for spending three early picks at RB.

## 3. Positional scarcity in this format (10 teams, 2 FLEX, full PPR)

### 3a. Replacement levels — derived for this lineup

Weekly starters league-wide: 10 QB, 20 RB, 20 WR, 10 TE, 20 FLEX. In full PPR the FLEX skews WR/pass-catching RB; assuming FLEX splits roughly 10 RB / 10 WR (TE rarely flexed), starter counts are **~RB30, ~WR30, TE10, QB10**. Replacement level ≈ **RB31 / WR31 / TE11 / QB11**.

| Format | RB starters | WR starters | TE / QB replacement | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10-team, 2 RB / 2 WR / 1 FLEX (common) | ~21 | ~26 (3 WR) or ~21 | TE11 / QB11 | Replacement RB is RB21 — shallow; "usable WRs sit on waivers" | Draft Sharks 10-team PPR guide [S29]; FantasyPros VBD [S30]
| **This league: 10-team, 2 RB / 2 WR / 2 FLEX** | **~30** | **~30** | TE11 / QB11 | RB/WR demand equals a 12-team single-flex league, but QB/TE stay 10-deep |
| 12-team, 2 RB / 3 WR / 1 FLEX | ~30 | ~42 | TE13 / QB13 | Classic format most studies use |

**Consequences (derivation, not an external study):**
1. RB and WR are each **~45% scarcer** than in a standard 10-team single-flex league (30 starters vs ~21). Do not import "10-team = wait on RB, WRs are on waivers" heuristics [S29]; those assume one flex.
2. QB and TE are **not** scarcer than a normal 10-team league: only 10 start, so the QB11/TE11 on waivers is a meaningfully better player than the QB13/TE13 a 12-team drafter is stuck with. That lowers early-QB and mid-TE value relative to the 12-team studies.
3. Two FLEX spots mean the 5th- and 6th-best RB/WR on your roster start every week. Depth at RB/WR produces points directly; QB2/TE2/DST2 produce none.

### 3c. Full PPR effect
Full PPR adds a point per reception, which (a) compresses the RB advantage from touchdowns/rushing volume, (b) lifts target-hog WRs and pass-catching RBs, and (c) inflates TE reception-driven scoring — McBride's record **126 catches** alone were 126 PPR points in 2025 [S33]. In the fantasysquawk 2015–2025 sample, WRs already hit more and bust less than RBs in Round 1 [S2]; full PPR pushes that further toward WR at the top of drafts. RBs who catch 50+ passes (Gibbs, Bijan, Achane, McCaffrey, Cook-type profiles) retain first-round value because they get both buckets.

## 4. QB timing: 4-pt vs 6-pt pass TD, 10 teams

| Evidence | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| ADP cost of QB1 vs QB12 (2025 drafts) | **QB1 = pick 23 overall; QB12 = pick 102** | Draft Sharks / PFN [S5][S6] |
| Hit rate (QB1-12 finish) for QBs drafted mid vs rounds 9–11 | **~50% both** — same success rate for ~80 picks less capital | [S5][S6] |
| 2025 QB1 | Josh Allen **21.4 PPG**, 14 rushing TD (3rd-most in NFL), QB1 for the 4th time in 6 seasons | ESPN Bowen tiers [S34] |
| 2025 context | **No QB reached 23+ PPG** for the first time in five years; top-3 QB scoring lowest in 13 years; fewest pass attempts since 2011; highest scramble rate in recent history | Fantasy Footballers 25 QB stats [S35] |
| Rushing premium (2025) | Five QBs with ≥5 rush att/game (Hurts, Daniels, Jackson, Allen, Nix) averaged **21.6 PPG** | ESPN [S34] |
| Rushing premium (long-run) | **30 of 43 (70%)** QB seasons with ≥80 carries since 2010 finished top-12 in PPG | RotoWire [S36] |
| Rushing vs pocket, 2025 | Rushing QBs **20.11 PPG** vs pocket passers **15.34** (Δ4.77); weekly SD 6–8 vs 8–11 | ParlaySavant [S37] — **low-authority source; treat as UNVERIFIED** |
| Lamar Jackson 2025 | **16.5 PPG**, 13 games (injury) | ESPN [S34] |
| Jalen Hurts 2025 | 8 rush TD (fewest inside-5 carries since 2021) but **20.3 PPG Weeks 10–16** | ESPN [S34] |
| 6-pt vs 4-pt magnitude | A 10-TD gap between QBs = **+20 pts/season ≈ 1.2 PPG** extra in 6-pt leagues; only 8 QB seasons of 40+ pass TD in the prior decade | PFF 6-pt adjustment [S38] |
| 6-pt strategy | Early QB becomes "viable" because QB is the highest-scoring position; but "core principles stay intact: wait on QBs and build anchored rosters" | FTN 6-pt mock [S39]; FantasyOwner [S40] |

**Analysis for this league:**
- **4-pt league (pick 2):** The QB1-QB12 gap (~4–7 PPG) is smaller than RB8-RB36 (6–7.5) and WR8-WR36 (5.5–6.5) [S5], and with only 10 starters your replacement is QB11, not QB13. The break-even for taking Allen at his ~pick-23 cost is roughly a 4-PPG edge over the QB you could get at pick ~100; 2025 delivered less than that (Allen 21.4 vs QB12 ~17–18 PPG, UNVERIFIED exact QB12). **Wait: target rounds 7–10, prioritize rushing floor** (≥5 att/game profiles).
- **6-pt league (pick 4):** Each TD is +2, so a 35-TD passer gains +70 pts/season (~4 PPG) over 4-pt scoring, and the top-vs-QB12 gap widens by roughly (TD gap × 2)/17 ≈ +1.2 PPG per 10 TDs [S38]. Combined with the rushing premium, an Allen/Daniels/Hurts-type is worth about one round earlier than in 4-pt, **but still not a round 1–3 pick in a 10-team league** with double flex. Practical rule: in 6-pt, take a rushing QB if one is there at the R5/R6 turn (picks 44/57); otherwise apply the same late-QB plan.
- Do **not** roster two QBs in either league (see §9).

**Analysis for this league:** In a 10-team league only 10 TEs start, so the streaming pool (TE11+) is deeper than in 12-team studies, which trims the elite-TE edge from ~8.5 PPG to something smaller — perhaps 6–7 PPG (derivation, UNVERIFIED). That still exceeds the QB1-QB12 gap (4–7 PPG) at similar or lower draft cost (Bowers/McBride ~pick 16–18 vs Allen ~pick 23 in 2025 drafts). Full PPR magnifies the reception-heavy TE profile (McBride, Bowers). **Rule of thumb:** an elite TE is worth a late-2nd/3rd-round pick here (pick 19/22 or 24/37) *only if* both top-6 RBs are gone and no WR1-tier receiver remains; otherwise TE is the position to punt and stream, because the 2-FLEX lineup rewards RB/WR depth more than any single-slot position.

## 7. Pick 2 vs Pick 4 (10-team snake, 15 rounds)

### 7a. Turn structure

| Round | Pick 2 | Pick 4 | Gap to your next pick (P2 / P4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 4 | 17 / 13 |
| 2 | 19 | 17 | 3 / 7 |
| 3 | 22 | 24 | 17 / 13 |
| 4 | 39 | 37 | 3 / 7 |
| 5 | 42 | 44 | 17 / 13 |
| 6 | 59 | 57 | 3 / 7 |
| 7 | 62 | 64 | 17 / 13 |
| 8 | 79 | 77 | 3 / 7 |
| 9 | 82 | 84 | 17 / 13 |
| 10 | 99 | 97 | 3 / 7 |
| 11 | 102 | 104 | 17 / 13 |
| 12 | 119 | 117 | 3 / 7 |
| 13 | 122 | 124 | 17 / 13 |
| 14 | 139 | 137 | 3 / 7 |
| 15 | 142 | 144 | — |

- Pick 2 is effectively a near-turn slot: picks come in **pairs 3 apart** (19/22, 39/42, 59/62…) with 17-pick waits between pairs. Pick 4 has **7-apart pairs** (17/24, 37/44…) with 13-pick waits.
- Consequence: from pick 2 you can plan two-player "packages" (e.g., WR + RB at 19/22) with near-certainty about what will be there for the second pick; from pick 4 you see more of the board between picks but must decide earlier in each round whether a tier will survive 6 picks.

**Slot-specific analysis:**
- **Pick 2 (4-pt league):** You are guaranteed one of the two consensus top RBs (Gibbs/Bijan) or Chase. Given §2 (one elite RB + WR-heavy is the highest-equity shape) and §3 (full PPR favors dual-threat RBs), take the **best remaining of Gibbs/Bijan** at 2. Then at 19/22 you are picking at the 2/3 turn: the data supports **WR + WR** or **WR + elite TE** there (three WRs by R6 is the strongest documented pattern [S13][S14]); the second RB should come at 39/42 only if a top-10 RB falls, otherwise in the 59–82 range (rounds 6–9), matching the RB2-after-R7 finding [S15][S16].
- **Pick 4 (6-pt league):** Two of Gibbs/Bijan/Chase will be gone. If a top-2 RB is there, take him (same logic). If not, **Nacua or Chase** over McCaffrey (age-30 RB in 2026 — see §8) or JSN. Then 17/24 offers the chance at an RB from the Taylor/Achane/Cook/Henry tier at 17 and a WR1 at 24; that 7-pick gap makes it the natural place for a **Hero RB** if you opened WR. The 6-pt scoring lifts a rushing QB by ~1 round; the 44/57 pair (R5/R6) is the earliest the evidence supports.
- Do not treat slot as a reason to change structure: both slots should exit round 6 with **1 RB + 4 WR/TE** or **2 RB + 3 WR** and never 3+ RBs [S14].

**Analysis for this league (15 rounds, 6 bench + IR, no K):** Starters consume 9 of 15 picks (QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, TE, 2 FLEX, DST). That leaves **6 bench picks**. Recommended allocation: **3 RB + 2 WR + 1 swing (RB/WR/TE)**, zero QB2, zero DST2. Use the IR slot aggressively (draft one injured player with a Week 6–10 return if available in round 13–15). The dead-slot cost of a QB2 or TE2 is large here because two FLEX spots mean every bench RB/WR is one injury from starting.

## Actionable rules for this league

1. **Round 1: take the best available of Gibbs / Bijan / Chase / Nacua, RB first if a top-2 RB is there.** Round-1 RBs and WRs both hit top-12 only ~50% of the time (RB 54%, WR 48%) and bust ~36% [S1][S2]; in full PPR the elite dual-threat RBs earn both buckets, and one elite RB is the common thread in 2024 (Barkley 48% advance) and 2025 (Henry-anchored BBM VI winner) [S19][S20]. At pick 4, if both Gibbs and Bijan are gone, take Chase or Nacua over McCaffrey (age-30 RB; production drops ~15% at 28→29 and again after) [S54][S57].
2. **Exit Round 6 with at least three WRs (or 3 WR/TE) and no more than two RBs.** "3 WRs through Round 6" was near-optimal in every BBM year; no roster with 3+ RBs in the first five rounds advanced above 15% (baseline 16.7%) [S13][S14]. Championship high-stakes teams carry ≥4 WR and 2 RB through eight rounds [S23].
3. **Buy your RB2/RB3/RB4 in Rounds 6–11 (picks 57–104), not Rounds 3–5.** RB2 after Round 7 is the documented strong bet and 4 RBs through Round 10 was top-2 in each of 2022–2024 [S13][S15][S16]. Late RBs produced RB13 Irving, Chase Brown, and Hubbard in 2024 [S25]; the 2025 lull was driven by an outlier-healthy RB year (top-2-round RBs missed ~1 game each vs ~3 in 2023–24) [S53] that should regress.
4. **Treat this format as 12-team scarcity at RB/WR, not 10-team.** With 2 RB + 2 WR + 2 FLEX, ~30 RBs and ~30 WRs start weekly (replacement ≈ RB31/WR31), the same as a 12-team single-flex league; the "usable WRs on waivers in 10-team" heuristic [S29] does not apply (derivation, §3a). Draft 6–7 WRs and 5–6 RBs across 15 picks.
5. **Wait on QB in the 4-pt league (pick 2): target Rounds 7–10.** QB1 cost pick 23 vs QB12 at pick 102 in 2025 drafts, yet mid- and late-round QBs hit QB1-12 at the same ~50% rate [S5][S6]; the QB1-QB12 gap (4–7 PPG) is smaller than RB8-36 (6–7.5) or WR8-36 (5.5–6.5) [S5], and here replacement is QB11, not QB13.
6. **In the 6-pt league (pick 4), move QB up about one round — not more.** A 10-TD edge is worth only ~+20 pts/season (~1.2 PPG) in 6-pt scoring [S38]; the earliest the evidence supports is the 44/57 turn (R5/R6), and only for a rushing QB.
7. **Whichever QB you take, require a rushing floor.** 70% of QB seasons with 80+ carries since 2010 finished top-12 in PPG [S36]; the five QBs with ≥5 rush att/game in 2025 averaged 21.6 PPG vs Allen's 21.4 as QB1 [S34]; in a year where no QB hit 23 PPG and pass attempts were the fewest since 2011 [S35], rushing is the only reliable separator.
8. **Never draft a second QB, second TE, or second DST.** One-QB leagues have far more startable QBs than rosters need; a QB2 can only replace one player while a bench RB/WR can start in two FLEX slots [S65][S66]. Use the six bench spots as roughly 3 RB / 2 WR / 1 swing (§9).
9. **Elite TE only at 19/22 (pick 2) or 24/37 (pick 4) if the top-2 RBs and WR1 tier are gone; otherwise punt TE and stream.** TE1 tier beats streamers by ~8.5 PPG in 12-team math [S32] (McBride 18.6 PPG in 2025 [S33]), but with only 10 TE starters the streaming pool is deeper and 2025's TE3/TE6 were TD-luck outcomes [S33]. In a 2-FLEX lineup, a WR/RB of the same ADP starts too.
10. **DST in Round 15 only; pick by Week 1–2 matchup and stream thereafter.** A top-3 ADP DST finishes #1 just ~3.8% of the time and preseason rank barely correlates with finish [S43][S44]; points allowed / opponent quality is the predictable signal [S45].
11. **Age tiebreaks: fade RBs entering age 28+, ignore WR age until 30.** Top-20 RB seasons drop from 22–23 per year at ages 27–28 to 11 at 29 and 8 at 30 [S54]; elite RB PPG falls 16.8% (26→27), 8.4% (27→28), 15.3% (28→29) [S57]. Elite WRs at 30 averaged 16.4 PPG with no cliff year since 2011 [S60].
12. **Rookie tiebreaks: prefer Round-1/2 NFL-capital rookie RBs to rookie WRs at the same ADP.** First-round rookie RBs return immediate RB1 value 25.5% of the time and RB2+ 55% within three seasons; first-round rookie WRs are 27.9% to finish top-24 and 11.6% top-12 as rookies, and Day-2 rookie WRs only 13.5% top-36 [S7][S11].
13. **Late rounds (11–15): "handcuff-plus" backups with standalone roles and one handcuff for your own RB1 — no pure clipboard handcuffs.** Handcuffs only gain value when something bad happens, and each costs a slot that could hold a player who gains value on his own [S62]; the 2024 late-RB hits were timeshare backs who took over (Irving, Chase Brown) [S25][S26].
14. **Use the pick-2 pairing (3 picks apart) to plan two-player packages; use the pick-4 spacing (7 apart) to take the scarcer tier first.** Pick 2 sees only 2 players leave between 19 and 22, so plan WR+WR or WR+TE there; pick 4 must decide at 17 whether the RB tier (Taylor/Achane/Cook/Henry) survives to 24 — it usually will not, so take the RB at 17 and the WR at 24 if you opened WR [S49][S50][S51].
15. **Draft for the tail, not the average.** League winners were 39% RB / 30% WR / 11% QB / 11% TE from 2017–2024 [S22]; the 2025 BBM VI champion came from a WR-heavy Hero-RB build even in a year the average Zero-RB roster lost ground [S20][S21]. Accept the variance of a WR-heavy start with one elite RB and late RB volume — that is where titles came from in 2023, 2024, and 2025.
