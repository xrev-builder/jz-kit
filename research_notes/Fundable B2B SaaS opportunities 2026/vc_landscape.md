## 1. 2025-2026 seed/pre-seed volumes, round sizes and valuations (AI vs non-AI)

### Takeaway
In 2026 venture money is extremely concentrated in AI. AI took about 64% of global VC in Q3 2026 and about 86% of US deal value in H1 2026, most of it in mega-rounds. Early-stage deal counts are falling while dollars hold flat: Carta logged about 11,500 pre-seed instruments in Q2 2026, versus 14,825 a year earlier. The median seed is about $4-4.5M at about $24M post-money. AI companies command a premium (about $25M median seed valuation; AI infra about $66M post) and take about half of pre-seed dollars. Non-AI B2B SaaS is being raised into a shrinking pool of deals.

### Cited Findings
**Macro / all-stage (2026)**
- PitchBook-NVCA Q2 2026 (H1): AI companies took $355.9B, about 86% of all US venture dollars in H1 2026. US H1 deal value was $412.7B. — [SiliconANGLE, Jul 9 2026](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/09/pitchbook-us-venture-funding-hits-412-7b-first-half-ai-deals-dominate/)
- PitchBook-NVCA Q1 2026: US venture funding hit a record $267B in Q1, dominated by OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI deals. — [SiliconANGLE, Apr 3 2026](https://siliconangle.com/2026/04/03/pitchbook-us-venture-funding-surges-record-267b-openai-anthropic-xai-dominate-ai-deals/)
- PitchBook-NVCA Q3 2026 (released about Oct 8, 2026): US deal value reached about $515.8B YTD through 9 months, about 44% above the prior annual record. Q3 alone fell about 40% to $98.4B, mostly because of fewer venture-growth mega-rounds. OpenAI and Anthropic together raised more than $200B in H1. — [SiliconANGLE (via search summary)](https://siliconangle.com/?p=849755). A figure of 82.7% of Q3 funding going to AI appears only in a LinkedIn post and is unverified.
- Crunchbase Q3 2026: global venture was about $159B, of which AI took about $102B (64%). A record 27 rounds were $1B+. AI's share was down from the prior two quarters but 14 percentage points above Q3 2025. — [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-north-america-startup-funding-falls-ai-exits-data/); [AI Weekly summary](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/crunchbase-logs-27-billion-dollar-rounds-in-q3-2026-as-ai-grabs-102b-or-64-of)
- Crunchbase Q3 2026, North America: seed, angel and pre-seed funding was at least $5B (preliminary). AI took about two-thirds of all NA funding. Asia seed and angel totalled $4.9B, the majority to AI. Global seed was about $13B per a secondary summary, with about $2.6B of that in seed-labelled rounds of $100M+ (unverified). — [Crunchbase NA](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-north-america-startup-funding-falls-ai-exits-data/); [Crunchbase Asia](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-asia-startup-funding-near-record-ai/); [ValueAddVC pulse](https://valueaddvc.com/pulse/crunchbase-q3-2026-record-billion-dollar-rounds-2026)

**Pre-seed (Carta, 2026)**
- Carta State of Pre-Seed Q2 2026: US startups raised $3.19B across about 11,500 SAFEs and notes. Q2 2025 was $3.22B across 14,825 instruments. Dollars were about flat while deal count fell about 22% YoY. — [Carta](https://carta.com/data/state-of-pre-seed-q2-2026/)
- AI companies captured about half of all pre-seed dollars in 2026 YTD. A secondary guide citing Carta puts it at 48.6% for H1 2026. — [Carta](https://carta.com/data/state-of-pre-seed-q2-2026/); [Causo H1 2026 report](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/h1-2026-state-of-seed-report)
- For SAFEs over $2.5M, the median valuation cap was $35M in Q2 2026, up 40% YoY, and the 75th percentile approached $60M. — [Carta](https://carta.com/data/safe-valuation-caps-q2-2026/)

**Seed (Carta, 2026)**
- Carta's April 9, 2026 benchmark (1,000+ VC rounds over the prior 6 months) puts the median seed valuation for AI at $25M. — [Carta](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-vc-round-benchmarks-seed-series-a-b-2026/)
- Carta, May 2026: AI infrastructure startups raise a median $13M seed at a $66M post-money. — [Carta "The AI Infra trade"](https://carta.com/data/newsletter-the-ai-infra-trade/)
- All seed rounds, Carta 6-month benchmark to July 2026 (secondary citation): median $4.1M raised, $24.3M post-money, 18% dilution. — [Flowjam](https://flowjam.com/blog/seed-round-valuation-2025-complete-founders-guide). A conflicting secondary citation of Carta Q2 2026 gives $4.5M on $23.9M post with 19.4% dilution. — [WaveUp](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/)
- AI application-layer seed: $4-5M raised on $20-22M post-money, attributed to Carta's Peter Walker by a secondary blog and not confirmed on Carta's site. — [Flowjam](https://flowjam.com/blog/seed-round-valuation-2025-complete-founders-guide)

**Shutdowns**
- SimpleClosure H1 2026: AI was 14.4% of closures it handled, and AI's share is falling. Most shutdown volume is pre-AI-era software companies running out of runway. — [SimpleClosure](https://simpleclosure.com/blog/insights/state-of-shutdowns-h1-2026)

### Inferences
- Barbell market. Mega-rounds dominate dollars, while the pre-seed deal count is down about 22% YoY. More capital is going into fewer, larger early checks. The fact that the median cap on large SAFEs (over $2.5M) jumped 40% suggests "hot" AI teams raise big, highly priced pre-seeds, while median and non-AI companies face a thinner market.
- A non-AI B2B SaaS seed is plausibly priced at or below the all-seed median (about $24M post). AI infra sits about 2.7x higher ($66M). The premium is real for infra and smaller for application-layer AI ($20-22M if the Walker figure holds).
- SimpleClosure's data implies the "zombie" wave is mostly 2020-2022 vintage SaaS, not AI wrappers yet. Wrapper failures may lag.

## 3. Saturated / commoditized categories

### Takeaway
There is concrete evidence of crowding in AI SDRs and AI note-takers. In AI SDRs, leaders face churn allegations and have repositioned to hybrid copilots. In AI note-takers, a handful of scaled leaders (Granola, Otter, Fireflies) plus platform bundling and new litigation leave little room for new seed entrants. Generic "AI wrapper" skepticism is widely reported, but most quantified claims come from low-quality blogs.

  - Sources: [Salesmotion](https://salesmotion.io/blog/ai-sdr-claims-scrutiny-what-buyers-should-verify); [deathtocoldemails](https://deathtocoldemails.com/ai-sdr-post-mortem/); [toarn Q2 2026](https://toarn.com/insights/11x-ai)

## 4. Vertical AI / services-as-software / agentic workflow thesis and underpenetrated verticals

### Takeaway
The thesis, as articulated by Sequoia ("Services: The New Software") and Bessemer ("Building Vertical AI", Jan 2026), is that AI lets startups sell the work itself and capture labor or outsourcing budgets, which are far larger than software budgets, in verticals where outsourcing already exists. 2026 seed activity is visible in accounting/tax, insurance and construction. YC is pointing at compliance, defense, aging care and physical-world operations.

### Cited Findings
- Sequoia argues that autopilots capture the work budget, that founders should start where outsourcing already exists, and that the customer buys the outcome directly. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)
- Bessemer says vertical AI founders are broadly adopting usage- and output-based pricing, often with a base subscription. It uses EvenUp (per completed demand letter) as an example and puts AI gross margins at about 50-60%, versus 80-90% for classic SaaS. — [BVP Vertical AI book](https://www.bvp.com/assets/uploads/2026/01/BUILDING-VERTICAL-AI_PDF_BESSEMER_VENTURE_PARTNERS_BOOK_JANUARY_2026.pdf); [BVP pricing playbook](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-ai-pricing-and-monetization-playbook)
- **Insurance:** AI insurance startups raised $2.68B across 69 rounds from Jan to Sep 2026. 22 rounds were seed or pre-seed and 31 were $10M or less. — [Botmemo](https://botmemo.com/ai-in-insurance)
- **Accounting/tax (most active seed category found):**
  - Juno (AI tax prep for SMB accounting firms): $12M seed
  - Viewz: $7M seed, led by Ibex Investors and FLINT Capital
  - Arito: $6M seed, led by Amplify Partners
  - Source: [Preuve](https://preuve.ai/blog/vertical-ai-startup-ideas-2026); [newmarketpitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/vertical-ai-list-deals). Aggregator data; verify each round.
- **Construction:** six contech startups raised $126M in early 2026, including Enlaye ($5M seed, AI risk lifecycle) and OnSite ($1.3M seed). — [Preuve](https://preuve.ai/blog/vertical-ai-startup-ideas-2026)
- YC Fall 2026 asks point to underserved verticals: defense, aging population, compliance infra and physical-world operating systems. The Summer 2026 list includes vertical AI for legacy industries and agriculture. — [Modelence](https://modelence.com/yc-rfs-fall-2026); [RoundFunded](https://www.roundfunded.com/blogs/yc-summer-2026-request-for-startups-15-categories)
- Investors interviewed by Crunchbase cite accounting, ITSM and ERP as vertical app-layer winners. — [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/crunchbase-predicts-vcs-expect-more-funding-ai-ipo-ma-2026-forecast/)

### Inferences
- Underpenetrated targets that look fundable include:
  - Regulated, document-heavy verticals with existing outsourcing (insurance ops, tax/accounting, compliance)
  - Physical-world operations (construction, logistics, field services)
  - Public sector and defense
  - Eldercare
- Logistics showed no 2026 seed data in this search, which could mean either white space or a gap in the search.
- Legal (EvenUp, Harvey) and customer support (Sierra, Decagon) are more mature or crowded vertical-AI categories at this point. This is an inference from their prominence as reference examples.

### Gaps
- No systematic penetration data was found, such as AI adoption by vertical or count of funded startups per vertical. Logistics, healthcare RCM, property management and trades were not specifically quantified.
- AI-enabled services roll-ups (PE-style acquire-and-automate) appear in discourse, but no primary fund thesis was retrieved.

## 5. Seat-based vs outcome-based pricing and what investors reward

### Takeaway
AI-native vendors are moving to usage, outcome or hybrid pricing, with per-resolution customer-support agents as the canonical example. Incumbents layer outcome fees on top of seats. Pure outcome pricing is less common than the discourse suggests: ICONIQ reportedly puts it at about 23% of respondents. Investors reward pricing that captures labor budgets, but they also scrutinize the lower gross margins that come with AI (about 50-60%).

### Cited Findings
- Intercom Fin charges $0.99 per outcome on every plan, on top of $29-132 per seat per month for human agents. Seat prices reportedly dropped in 2026 while the outcome fee stayed the same. An "outcome" includes cases where the customer stops asking after Fin's answer. — [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/learning-center/intercom-pricing); [Usercall](https://www.usercall.co/post/intercom-pricing)
- Sierra charges per outcome, with a rate reported at about $1.50 per resolution and $0 on failure. Other analysis says Sierra publishes neither the rate nor the scoring rule, so the sources conflict. — [BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/sierra-per-resolution-economics-vertical-ai-agents-2026); [Aissist](https://aissist.io/insights/sierra-ai-pricing)
- Incumbents (Zendesk, LivePerson, Cognigy) layer outcome AI pricing on top of seats. AI natives (Sierra, Decagon, Fin standalone) charge per resolution only. — [Aissist](https://aissist.io/insights/sierra-ai-pricing)
- ICONIQ State of AI 2026 (via a critical newsletter) puts outcome pricing at about 23% of respondents (multi-select). The newsletter argues that per-minute and per-document fees are not true outcome pricing. — [The Verticalist / Euclid](https://insights.euclid.vc/p/vertical-ai-outcome-pricing)
- Seat-based pricing reportedly fell from 21% to 15% of SaaS companies in a year, but no primary source is given. — [Substack](https://bhavishyapandit9.substack.com/p/tsaas-pricing-ai)
- Bain 2026 benchmark (secondary citation): AI customer service payback is about 4.1 months. — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-unit-economics-pricing-margins-services-2026-framework)
- Bessemer: AI gross margins are about 50-60%, versus 80-90% for SaaS. 2025 "Supernovas" ran at about 25% gross margin and "Shooting Stars" at about 60%. — [BVP](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-ai-pricing-and-monetization-playbook); [BVP State of AI 2025 mirror](https://openlinksw.com/data/html/bessemer-ventures-ai-2025-report-claude.html)
- Public markets punished seat exposure. Atlassian reportedly fell about 35% on its first-ever enterprise seat decline (Feb 2026). — [Taskade](https://www.taskade.com/blog/saaspocalypse-explained)

### Inferences
- A fundable 2026 pricing story is a hybrid: a platform fee plus per-task or per-outcome fees tied to a labor budget, with a credible path to 60%+ gross margins. Pure seat pricing on horizontal tools is viewed as structurally exposed.
- Outcome pricing works best where the outcome is objectively measurable, such as a resolved ticket, a filed claim or a completed letter. That favors narrow vertical workflows.

### Gaps
- The primary ICONIQ State of AI 2026, OpenView-style pricing surveys, and a16z's own pricing posts were not retrieved.
- No data was found on how seed investors specifically value outcome-priced versus seat-priced ARR (for example, multiple differentials).
