# Non-Debt (Equity and Equity-Like) Funding Pathways for Early-Stage Founders, 2025-2026

Research date: 2026-10-20. Method note: WebFetch could not resolve several primary domains (ycombinator.com, carta.com, sec.gov returned DNS errors), so many figures below come from search-result summaries of primary pages or from secondary sources that cite Carta, PitchBook or Kingscrowd. Secondary or blog sources are flagged. Treat 2026 numbers from blogs (waveup, causo, mean.ceo, valueaddvc, flowjam) as unverified compilations.

## Angel investors and angel networks/syndicates

### Takeaway
Individual angels typically write $10k-$100k checks, with medians cited at roughly $10k-$50k depending on the dataset. Syndicates lower the per-member minimum to roughly $1k-$10k while letting a lead aggregate a larger single cap-table line. The data here is mostly from blogs or vendors and the figures conflict, because they measure different things (individual checks, per-round totals, or different samples).

### Cited Findings
- Typical direct angel checks: $25,000-$100,000 per deal. — [Angel Investors Network, 2026 guide](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/angel-investing/angel-investing-guide-2026) (vendor/blog)
- 2026 benchmark: median individual checks about $25k-$50k, with a full range of $10k to $2M. — [mean.ceo angel check-size benchmarks 2026](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=10271) (blog, unverified)
- A VentureSouth analysis of over 1,000 angel transactions found a median individual investment of $10,000. — cited via [AngelBacked, average angel check size](https://www.angelbacked.co/blog/average-angel-investor-check-size)
- Another summary claims the 2025 median angel check was $37,000 per investor and $427,000 per round. This conflicts with the VentureSouth median above, probably because the samples differ. — [AngelBacked, State of Angel Investing 2025](https://www.angelbacked.co/blog/state-of-angel-investing-2025)
- The mode of angel checks sits well below the mean, because a small number of high-net-worth angels write six-figure checks. "Average" figures that blend solo angels with pooled syndicates are misleading. — [AngelBacked](https://www.angelbacked.co/blog/average-angel-investor-check-size)
- Syndicate participation often starts at $1,000-$5,000 per member. Platform syndicate members or first-time angels commonly put in about $5k-$10k. — [Angel Investors Network minimums 2025](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/capital-raising/angel-investor-minimum-investment-real-2025-numbers); [metal.so angel syndicate vs seed VC 2025](https://www.metal.so/collections/angel-syndicate-vs-seed-vc-2025-check-sizes-dilution-guide)

### Inferences
- Angels are usually the first outside money for venture-track companies, and in practice they come in through SAFEs at the caps described in the Instruments section. A typical angel-only pre-seed of $250k-$1M therefore needs about 5-40 individual checks, or one syndicate lead.
- Syndicates (AngelList-style SPVs) suit founders who want many small checks without adding many cap-table lines. Local angel groups suit founders outside major hubs and companies in non-software sectors.

### Gaps
- I could not verify specific terms for Hustle Fund or Angel Squad (check sizes, membership) or AngelList's current syndicate fee and carry terms. No primary source was retrieved.
- I found no Angel Capital Association (ACA) 2025 data on angel-group median round sizes or valuations.
- I found no data on how long angel rounds take to close.

## Pre-seed and seed VC: round sizes and valuations (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
On Carta, pre-seed is about 90% SAFEs, with median post-money caps of roughly $7.5M (under $250k raised), $10M ($250k-$1M) and $15M ($1M-$2.5M) in 2025. Median priced seed rounds are about $3-4M raised at roughly $16-20M pre-money ($24M post-money on Carta, 2025-mid-2026), with about 18-20% dilution. Valuations are at record highs, but capital is concentrated (especially in AI). Bridge rounds and long gaps before Series A are common.

### Cited Findings
- Carta's 2025 review: $10.4B was raised across 50,316 SAFEs and convertible notes by US startups on Carta. Median post-money SAFE caps were about $10M for $250k-$1M rounds and about $15M for $1M-$2.5M rounds. — [Carta State of Pre-Seed 2025 in review](https://carta.com/sg/en/data/state-of-pre-seed-2025/) (via search summary)
- Q2 2025: the median cap for SAFE rounds under $250k was $7.5M, up from $6.5M the prior quarter. In Q3 2025, caps stayed mostly flat except for rounds of $2.5M or more, where they rose. — [Carta State of Pre-Seed Q2 2025](https://carta.com/data/state-of-pre-seed-q2-2025/)
- Carta's Q2 2026 data release reports that caps on new pre-seed SAFEs "keep getting bigger" (headline only; I could not open the page to get the numbers). — [Carta, SAFE valuation caps Q2 2026](https://carta.com/data/safe-valuation-caps-q2-2026/)
- Carta Q3 2025: the median pre-money for new priced seed rounds was $16M, up 14% year over year. Carta's Q4 2025 median seed post-money was $24M. — cited via [waveup seed benchmarks 2026](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/) and the search summary (the pre-money and post-money figures are not directly comparable)
- Carta 2026 figure, cited secondarily: the median priced seed post-money was $24.3M in the six months to July 2026 (a record), with a median of $4.1M raised and 18% median dilution. — [waveup](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/); [flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/seed-round-valuation-2025-complete-founders-guide) (secondary)
- PitchBook-NVCA (as relayed by secondary sources): the US median seed pre-money was about $18.4M in Q1 2026, more than double 2021. The full-year 2025 median pre-money was $16.0M. The all-sector median seed deal size is about $3.0M, while other sources cite $3.8M (FY2025) or about $4M (Q4 2025). — [causo H1 2026 State of Seed](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/h1-2026-state-of-seed-report); [peony Q1 2026 benchmarks](https://peony.ink/blog/startup-fundraising-benchmarks-q1-2026) (secondary, inconsistent)
- AI vs non-AI seed pre-money in Q1 2026: about $18.7M vs $18.0M. — secondary, per the search summary of the [causo seed valuation benchmark 2026](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/seed-round-valuation-benchmark-2026)
- Pre-seed priced-deal medians: about $0.8M deal size and about $8M pre-money (a J.P. Morgan-based table relayed by a blog). Another source cites PitchBook at a $7.7M pre-seed median pre-money for Q4 2025. — [valueaddvc pre-seed vs seed vs Series A](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/pre-seed-vs-seed-vs-series-a) (secondary)
- Carta data: 46% of all seed deals were bridge rounds in Q1 2025, the highest bridge rate for any stage Carta has recorded. Series A deal count fell 79% from Q1 2022 to Q1 2025. — [Axios, "Venture capital is stalling at seed" (May 2025)](https://www.axios.com/2025/05/18/venture-capital-stalling-seed)
- Time from seed to Series A has stretched to about 2.1 years (Carta State of Seed 2025, cited secondarily). — [letsdatascience summary](https://letsdatascience.com/news/seed-market-concentrates-funding-among-few-startups-ecc46f40) (secondary)
- One commentary argues that Carta data over-indexes on San Francisco and AI cap tables, so it may overstate the broad US median. — per search summary of [mean.ceo](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=5728) (opinion)

### Inferences
- Rule-of-thumb dilution per round is about 15-25%. The 18% Carta median at seed is consistent with this.
- Headline medians mask wide dispersion. Non-AI companies, companies outside the Bay Area and NYC, and companies on non-Carta cap tables likely see lower caps and valuations than these medians.
- The high bridge-round share and the roughly 2-year seed-to-A gap mean founders should raise enough for 24+ months of runway at seed.

### Gaps
- I could not open the primary Carta or PitchBook-NVCA Q2/Q3 2026 reports. The 2026 numbers above are secondary.
- I found no reliable data on how long it takes to close a pre-seed or seed round (weeks or months from first meeting to wire).
- Crunchbase News 2025-2026 seed totals were not retrieved.

## Accelerators (YC, Techstars, 500 Global, Antler, Entrepreneur First)

### Takeaway
YC remains the top program: $500k for about 7% plus an MFN position, now with four batches a year and roughly 1% (or lower) acceptance. Techstars moved to a YC-like $220k structure in April 2025, and 500 Global's Flagship offers $150k for 6%. Antler and EF are "pre-team" or "pre-idea" programs that take more equity (about 8-12%) for less cash, in exchange for co-founder matching.

### Cited Findings
- **Y Combinator:** The standard deal is $500,000. $125,000 is on a post-money SAFE for 7%, and $375,000 is an uncapped MFN SAFE that takes the lowest-cap terms issued between the start of the batch and the next equity round. YC's example: at a $15M post-money cap, the $375k converts to about 2.5%, for roughly 9.5% total. YC charges no fees, sets no milestones, and gets pro rata rights. — [YC Deal page](https://ycombinator.com/deal) (via search summary); [thecondia](https://thecondia.com/yc-comibinator-500000-standard-deal/)
- Criticism: some investors say the uncapped MFN lets YC "bully its way" into future rounds and squeezes out angels and micro-VCs. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=273828)
- YC acceptance: YC does not publish official rates. Third-party estimates put Summer 2025 at about 0.6% (about 160-169 companies), with 20,000-40,000+ applications per cycle. YC now runs four batches a year: Spring 2025 had 144 companies, and Winter 2026 had 196 at an estimated ~1%. The older 1.5-2% figure is dated. — [wearefounders YC acceptance 2026](https://www.wearefounders.uk/y-combinator-acceptance-rate-2026/); [Rebel Fund](https://www.rebelfund.vc/blog-posts/y-combinator-vs-techstars-2025-acceptance-rates-equity-outcomes) (secondary estimates)
- **Techstars** (from April 17, 2025, starting with Fall 2025 programs): $220,000, made up of $200,000 on an uncapped MFN SAFE and $20,000 on a post-money Convertible Equity Agreement. Techstars receives 5% in common stock plus the future value of the MFN SAFE. — [Techstars Investment Terms Update](https://www.techstars.com/equity-back-guarantee-the-details); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2996173)
- Techstars Asia-Pacific terms conflict: one source gives a $100k MFN SAFE, another $120k. — [elev-x](https://elev-x.com/?p=6013); [startupcafe.ro](https://startupcafe.ro/investitii-de-220-000-dolari-prin-acceleratorul-american-techstars-care-a-sprijinit-si-antreprenori-romani-80181)
- **500 Global** Flagship Accelerator: $150,000 for 6%. — [500.co Flagship](https://500.co/founders/flagship)
- **Antler** (terms vary by region):
  - UK (from Autumn 2025): £500k package, with £125k for 8.5% equity plus an £85k convertible note at inception, and up to £330k pre-committed to the next round. — [Antler press release](https://www.antler.co/press-releases/antler-significantly-increases-investment-for-uks-best-tech-founders)
  - Other regions (per search summary of Antler regional pages): Singapore $150k for 10%; US $250k for 10% plus a $150k uncapped MFN SAFE; Australia AU$260k for 12% plus a disclosed AU$75k program fee. — [CB Insights Antler profile](https://www.cbinsights.com/investor/antler) and [Antler](https://www.antler.co/press-releases/antler-significantly-increases-investment-for-uks-best-tech-founders) (regional figures not individually verified)
- **Entrepreneur First:** Founders get an equity-free stipend or grant during the ideation and co-founder-matching phase, then EF invests up to about $250k once a company forms. One 2026 guide describes the US structure as a $125k post-money SAFE for 8% plus a $125k uncapped MFN SAFE (unverified against EF's official site). — [superscout EF profile](https://superscout.co/investor/entrepreneur-first); [causo EF guide 2026](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/how-to-apply-to-entrepreneur-first-2026)

### Inferences
- Effective price comparison (cash per 1% at the fixed-equity tranche): YC about $17.9k per 1%, 500 Global $25k per 1%, Antler UK about £14.7k per 1%. Techstars' fixed 5% comes mostly bundled with the MFN money.
- The main value of top accelerators is signaling, investor access (Demo Day) and network, not the cash. Lower-tier programs taking 6-10% may be expensive relative to the value they add.
- Antler and EF suit solo or pre-idea founders who need a co-founder. YC, Techstars and 500 suit teams that already exist.

### Gaps
- I found no official acceptance rates for Techstars, 500, Antler or EF. I could not access official EF terms.
- I found no outcome data (follow-on rates by program) from a primary source.

## Instruments: post-money SAFEs, convertible notes, priced rounds

### Takeaway
SAFEs dominate pre-seed: about 90% of Carta pre-seed rounds in Q1 2025 and about 92% of pre-priced rounds in Q3 2025. Most SAFEs carry a valuation cap and no discount. Where there is a discount, it is almost always 20%. Notes remain a minority, at a median interest rate of 7%. Seed is typically the first priced round, with about 18-20% dilution.

### Cited Findings
- SAFEs were a record 90% of all pre-seed rounds on Carta in Q1 2025, with convertible notes at 10%. SAFEs made up 82% of pre-seed dollars, because there were some large note deals. — [Carta State of Pre-Seed Q1 2025](https://carta.com/data/state-of-pre-seed-q1-2025/)
- About 92% of pre-priced rounds were on SAFEs in Q3 2025 (secondary attribution to Carta). — [SaaStr, 10 learnings from Carta data](https://www.saastr.com/the-state-of-seed-today-10-key-learnings-from-cartas-latest-data)
- In 2025, 61% of SAFEs used a cap with no discount. When a discount is present, it is 20% in 63% of cases. — secondary summary of Carta data via [Kruze Consulting pre-seed funding](https://kruzeconsulting.com/blog/preseed-funding/) (the Kruze table mixes sources, so treat with caution)
- The median convertible-note interest rate held at 7% in Q1 2025. — [Carta Q1 2025](https://carta.com/data/state-of-pre-seed-q1-2025/)
- Median priced-seed dilution is 18% (Carta 2026, secondary). — [waveup](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/)
- MFN mechanics (YC example): an uncapped MFN SAFE adopts the most favorable terms of later SAFEs issued before the priced round. — [YC Deal](https://ycombinator.com/deal)
- Techstars uses a "Post-Money Convertible Equity Agreement" for part of its deal, an example of newer convertible-equity variants. — [Techstars](https://www.techstars.com/equity-back-guarantee-the-details)

### Inferences
These points come from general knowledge of the standard YC SAFE documents. I could not re-fetch ycombinator.com/documents to verify them, so the writer should present them as standard definitions, not as sourced statistics.
- **Post-money SAFE:** The cap includes all SAFE money, so investor ownership = investment / post-money cap (for example, $1M on a $10M post cap = 10%). Founders bear all dilution from stacking SAFEs, and this is the key pitfall of raising many SAFEs.
- **Convertible note:** This is technically debt, with interest, a maturity date and often a cap and discount. It converts at the priced round, so maturity creates repayment or renegotiation risk. Founders generally prefer SAFEs, while some non-US jurisdictions and some investors still use notes.
- **Priced round:** Requires a set valuation, a preferred-stock term sheet (liquidation preference, typically 1x non-participating), board composition, pro rata and protective provisions. Legal costs and closing time are higher than for a SAFE.
- **Pro rata:** The right to maintain ownership in later rounds. In the YC standard documents, a SAFE pro rata side letter is optional.
- Stacked caps at the 2025 medians imply roughly 10% dilution per $1M at a $10M cap. Combined with a typical 15-20% option pool and 18% at seed, founders commonly hold about 55-65% after seed (illustrative, not sourced).

### Gaps
- I could not access YC's official SAFE user guide or Carta's full-year 2025 split of cap-only, cap-plus-discount and discount-only SAFEs.
- I found no current data on the prevalence of the pro rata side letter.
- I found no data on non-US instruments (UK ASA/SEIS-EIS-compliant agreements, KISS, Canadian SAFE variants).

## Equity crowdfunding (Wefunder, Republic, StartEngine, Crowdcube, Seedrs) and Reg CF / Reg A+ limits

### Takeaway
US Reg CF allows up to $5M per 12 months, and Reg A+ Tier 2 allows up to $75M (Tier 1 up to $20M, with state review). Typical Reg CF raises are small: the median successful raise is about $194k and the average about $572k. Total 2025 Reg CF volume was about $378M (Kingscrowd) to about $500M (CCA). Crowdfunding suits consumer brands and community-backed or local businesses better than deep-tech VC tracks.

### Cited Findings
- The 2021 SEC amendments raised the Reg CF limit from $1.07M to $5M per 12 months and the Reg A+ Tier 2 limit from $50M to $75M. They removed investment limits for accredited investors in Reg CF, and the non-accredited investor limit now uses the greater of annual income or net worth. Tier 1 allows up to $20M but requires state review. — [Crowdfund Insider exemptions explained](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=188205); [Barton Esq.](https://www.bartonesq.com/?p=2985); [StartEngine Reg A vs Reg D vs Reg CF](https://www.startengine.com/insights/private-market-education/investing-in-private-companies/reg-a-vs-reg-d-vs-reg-cf)
- Reg A+ Tier 2 caps non-accredited investors at 10% of the greater of income or net worth, unless the securities are listed. One source wrongly says there are no limits. — [Angel Investors Network Reg D vs A+ vs CF](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/regulatory-compliance/reg-d-vs-reg-a-vs-reg-cf-comparison)
- 2025 Kingscrowd data:
  - Reg CF raised $378.3M, up 11%, while the number of new offerings fell 29%. Total investment crowdfunding was $924.8M, up 58%, with Reg A+ at $546.6M. — [Angel Investors Network 2025 scorecard](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/startups/reg-cf-vs-reg-a-plus-2025-scorecard-924-million-crowdfunding-data); [Kingscrowd](https://kingscrowd.com/top-performing-states-in-equity-crowdfunding-2025-edition)
  - Of 1,189 Reg CF closings, 67.4% met their funding targets. The median successful raise was $194k and the average was $572k. — same sources
- CCA puts 2025 Reg CF at almost $500M, with 28% fewer offerings and a third consecutive year of declining offering counts. This conflicts with Kingscrowd's total. — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=257053)
- 2025 Reg CF dollars by platform (Kingscrowd, relayed): Wefunder about $109M across 367 deals, StartEngine about $89M, DealMaker about $66M, Republic about $20M. Wefunder claims about $983M cumulative since 2016. — [Angel Investors Network StartEngine vs Wefunder 2026](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/capital-raising/startengine-vs-wefunder-2026-equity-crowdfunding-platform-comparison) (promotional-style, approximate)
- UK: the CMA's provisional findings blocked the Crowdcube-Seedrs merger, citing a combined share of at least 90%, and the deal was abandoned in 2021. — [GOV.UK CMA](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/crowdcube-and-seedrs-abandon-merger-during-cma-investigation)
- Republic agreed to buy Seedrs for about $100M in December 2021 and completed the deal on September 1, 2022. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/12/01/republic-acquires-the-uks-seedrs-in-100m-deal-to-push-into-europe); [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/republic-completes-acquisition-of-seedrs/)

### Inferences
- Crowdfunding gives founders customer-evangelists and marketing value, plus control: investors are often pooled via a crowd SAFE or SPV, and Reg CF allows SPVs that keep the cap table to one line.
- Costs include platform fees (commonly cited at about 5-8% of funds raised plus other costs; not verified here), ongoing annual reporting (Form C-AR), and a public disclosure of financials.
- A large crowd cap table may concern later VCs, although SPVs mitigate this.
- The roughly $194k median means most Reg CF raises are seed or friends-and-community sized, not substitutes for a $3-4M VC seed.

### Gaps
- I could not access the sec.gov Reg CF page. The exact current non-accredited investor thresholds are not verified here: the search summary cites a $124,000 threshold, and older thresholds were $2,500 or 5%, so the 2025 inflation-adjusted figures need confirming. Financial-statement tiers (self-certified, reviewed, audited) are also unverified.
- I found no data on UK FCA retail investor limits (the "restricted investor" 10%-of-net-assets rule) or on Crowdcube's 2025 volumes.
- I found no EU ECSPR (€5M cap) source.

## Non-dilutive and founder-friendly alternatives (RBF, grants/SBIR, CVC, strategics, family offices, rolling funds, micro-VCs)

### Takeaway
Revenue-based financing (RBF) fits companies that already have recurring or e-commerce revenue. Repayment is typically capped at about 1.2-2.0x principal, paid from 2-8% of revenue (up to about 30% of weekly sales for e-commerce sweeps), with effective APRs of about 12-40%. RBF is non-dilutive but debt-like. SBIR/STTR was reauthorized in April 2026 through FY2031, after a lapse of about six months. Phase I is about $295k-$305k at NIH and NSF. CVC is active, with about 1,000 deals per quarter, and roughly 35% of US CVC deals are at seed.

### Cited Findings
- **RBF, Lighter Capital:**
  - One example uses a 1.5x cap with royalties of 6% of revenue, so a $600k advance means $900k repaid. Another source gives a 1.35-1.5x cap on $50k-$4M of funding, with payments of 2-8% of monthly revenue. One source says Lighter requires at least $200k ARR. — [Angel Investors Network RBF 2026](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/alternative-investments/revenue-based-financing-accredited-investors-explained-2026); [startupsavant](https://startupsavant.com/?p=19300) (secondary)
  - In 2026, Lighter secured access to $100M in new capital. — [heyfuturenexus](https://www.heyfuturenexus.com/lighter-capital-secures-access-to-100m-in-new-capital) (secondary)
- **RBF, Clearco:** Aimed at e-commerce. Pricing is a flat fee of 6-12.5%, repaid via a revenue sweep of up to about 30% per week, which works out to an effective annual cost of about 14-40%+. — [eightx Clearco review](https://eightx.co/blog/compare/reviews/clearco-for-ecommerce-review) (a CFO firm's opinion)
- **RBF, Pipe:** Prices recurring revenue at a discount rather than with a multiple cap, and targets SaaS and subscription businesses. — [mean.ceo RBF guide 2026](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=7273) (secondary)
- **RBF, general:** Caps cited at 1.2-1.5x or 1.35-2.0x. In one example, payoff in 22 months means an effective cost above 25%, while payoff in 48 months means about 12% APR. — [Angel Investors Network RBF 2026](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/alternative-investments/revenue-based-financing-accredited-investors-explained-2026)
- **SBIR/STTR:**
  - Authority lapsed from about October 1, 2025. The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act (S. 3971), signed April 13, 2026, extends the programs through September 30, 2031. — [Crowell & Moring](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/sbirsttr-programs-reauthorized-after-six-month-lapse); [grantedai](https://grantedai.com/blog/sbir-sttr-reauthorization-2026-signed-fy2031-strategic-breakthrough-award-30-million-application-caps-foreign-risk-screening-small-business-strategy)
  - Award ceilings: NSF Phase I $305,000 and Phase II $1,250,000. NIH Phase I (R43) up to $295,386 total costs. The standard ceilings did not change. The act adds a new Phase II "strategic breakthrough" tier of up to $30M at agencies spending more than $100M a year on SBIR, and lets agencies cap the number of proposals per company starting in FY2027. — [casrai NSF FY26 relaunch](https://www.casrai.org/news/nsf-sbir-sttr-fy26-relaunch); [opengrants](https://opengrants.io/sbir-phase-1-grant-amount-why-numbers-differ/)
- **Corporate VC:**
  - About 1,019 CVC deals in Q3 2025 and 965 in Q4 2025 (PitchBook data via Aranca), below pre-2022 levels. — [Aranca CVC Q4 2025](https://www.aranca.com/knowledge-library/special-reports/business-research/quarterly-spotlight-corporate-venture-capital-deal-analysis-q4-2025)
  - US CVCs made about 35% of their deals at seed between 2022 and 2025, compared with 24% for Canadian CVCs in 2025. — [Deloitte State of Corporate Venture 2026 (PitchBook data)](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone3/ca/en/docs/services/program/state-of-corporate-venture-2026-aoda-en.pdf)

### Inferences
- RBF is not equity, but it is not a conventional high-interest loan either. It only works once revenue is predictable, so it is a fit for profitable or near-profitable SaaS and e-commerce companies, not pre-revenue startups.
- SBIR is effectively non-dilutive equity-replacement capital for deep-tech, health and defense founders. Phase I plus Phase II (about $1.5M+) can rival a seed round with zero dilution, but timelines are months long and tied to solicitation cycles. The 2025-26 lapse shows political-continuity risk.
- CVC and strategic investors bring distribution and credibility. The trade-offs are possible signaling risk, rights of first refusal or information rights that deter acquirers, and slower decisions.
- Family offices increasingly invest directly at seed, with patient capital and flexible terms, but they are hard to reach without warm intros.
- Rolling funds and micro-VCs (funds under about $50-100M) write $100k-$1M first checks and often lead pre-seed rounds.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable 2025-26 data on rolling funds (AngelList's rolling-fund product status), family-office direct seed activity or micro-VC fund counts. I did not search for these due to the tool-call budget.
- I found no data on non-US grants (Innovate UK, EIC Accelerator, which I believe gives up to €2.5M grant plus equity; unverified).
- I found no official RBF provider rate sheets.

## Which path fits which business (venture-scale vs small/local/lifestyle)

### Takeaway
VC is a rare path. Kauffman estimates that only about 0.5% of entrepreneurs use VC, and that at least 83% use neither bank loans nor VC. Even among fast-growing Inc. 5000 firms, only about 6.5% raised VC. Most businesses should look to bootstrapping, customers, RBF (once revenue is recurring), grants, crowdfunding and community capital rather than VC or accelerators.

### Cited Findings
- 0.5% of entrepreneurs use VC, and at least 83% of entrepreneurs do not access bank loans or venture capital. — [Kauffman Capital Access Lab](https://kauffman.org/capital-access-lab/approach)
- VC supplied under 1% of all startup funding (Hellmann et al., 2011). The Kauffman Firm Survey put VC at 4.4% of funding across about 5,000 firms. Most startup capital comes from personal savings, friends and family, and loans. — [Kauffman, "Three facts about VC and entrepreneurship"](https://www.kauffman.org/currents/three-facts-you-probably-didnt-know-about-and-venture-capital-and-entrepreneurship/) (older data, pre-2015)

### Inferences
Suggested fit matrix for the writer:
- **Venture-scale tech** (large TAM, potential for 10x+ returns, software, AI or deep-tech): angels or syndicates, then a pre-seed SAFE, then a top accelerator, then a priced seed. Add SBIR for deep-tech. CVC or strategics come in later.
- **Consumer brand with a community:** Reg CF crowdfunding, angels, RBF once revenue exists, and family offices.
- **SaaS with steady ARR but moderate growth:** RBF, angels, and possibly micro-VCs or "indie" funds. A traditional VC seed is a poor fit.
- **Local, lifestyle or service businesses:** bootstrap, friends and family, community crowdfunding (Reg CF), and local grants. VC and accelerator equity are a poor fit, because VCs need outsize exits and taking 7-20% for capital the business cannot return at venture multiples misaligns incentives.
- **Research or science-based companies:** SBIR/STTR and other grants first (non-dilutive), then strategic or CVC money, then deep-tech VC.
