Method note: WebFetch could not reach any domain in this session (DNS failures on carta.com, ycombinator.com, nber.org), so every finding below comes from search-result snippets and summaries, not from reading the full pages. ## 1. Warm intros vs cold outreach: effectiveness and how to get intros

### Takeaway
Most VC deals are sourced through networks: in the largest academic survey, only about 10% of deals came inbound from founders. Warm intros get far higher reply and meeting rates than cold email, roughly 2x to 10x depending on the source. Hard, independent conversion data is scarce, and most numbers come from vendors.

### Cited Findings
- Gompers, Gornall, Kaplan & Strebulaev survey of 885 institutional VCs (NBER WP 22587, 2016; published in the JFE in 2020; **older data**) found: over 30% of deals came from professional networks, 30% were proactively self-generated by the firm, 20% were referred by other investors, 8% came from portfolio companies, and **only 10% came inbound from company management**. — [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/dec16/w22587.html); [Harvard Law School Forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2019/08/20/how-do-venture-capitalists-make-decisions)
- In the same survey, the median firm considered about 100 deals for every one it closed. The average firm screens about 200 companies and makes about 4 investments a year. — [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/dec16/w22587.html); [Harvard Law School Forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2019/08/20/how-do-venture-capitalists-make-decisions)
- Secondary sites cite "nearly 70% network-sourced." This looks like a loose sum of the survey's categories and is not stated in the paper, so don't use it. — [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/dec16/w22587.html)
- Cold email reply rates (vendor/newsletter data): one source says 5.1% for 2025 cold emails, with 95% getting no reply; another puts a well-crafted cold email to a VC at 1-2%, and about 1 meeting per 50-100 targeted cold contacts. — [Founders Capital Substack](https://founderscapital8.substack.com/p/cold-emails-to-vcs-have-a-95-non); [Metal](https://www.metal.so/collections/warm-intros-vs-cold-email-2025-conversion-data-referral-playbook)
- Warm intro reply rates vary widely by source: Draftboard says 30-40%; LaunchRock gives 50-80% response and 25-50% meeting rates ("rough patterns"); Fuzzy AI gives 13.1% warm vs 3.1% cold (a LinkedIn outreach context, not VC). — [Draftboard](https://www.draftboard.com/blog/warm-intros-vs-cold-email-benchmarks-2026); [LaunchRock](https://www.launchrock.com/library/expert-advice/warm-intro); [Fuzzy AI](https://getfuzzy.ai/blog/design-partner-outreach-linkedin)
- Metal platform data: investors respond to about 25% of intro requests, and a 20%+ opt-in rate counts as healthy for early-stage rounds. — [Metal intro benchmark](https://www.metal.so/data-desk/intro-response-benchmark); [Metal docs](https://docs.metal.so/content/pipeline-development/key-metrics.md)
- Counterpoint: AngelBacked reports suggestive evidence that cold-sourced VC investments may perform as well as or better than warm-sourced ones. It is based on a researcher's cold outreach to 187 VC firms, and the article says causation isn't proven. — [AngelBacked](https://www.angelbacked.co/blog/cold-email-vs-warm-intro-vc-returns-research)
- Ways to get intros, per YC's advice for its companies: investor demand clusters around Demo Day, so accelerators work as an intro channel. — [YC Blog](https://blog.ycombinator.com/fundraising-advice-for-yc-companies)

### Inferences
- Because 20% of deals come through other investors and 8% through portfolio companies, the best warm paths are (a) founders the VC has already backed and (b) angels or co-investors who already back you. A portfolio-founder referral likely beats a generic "connector" intro.
- Cold outreach isn't worthless (about 10% of deals), but founders need far higher volume and a sharper hook: traction, a notable customer, or a specific thesis fit.
- The "double opt-in" norm (the connector asks the investor first) explains why only about 25% of intro requests get accepted. Founders should give connectors a short, forwardable blurb.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, peer-reviewed 2025-2026 study comparing warm and cold conversion all the way to a term sheet. All conversion percentages come from vendors.
- I couldn't retrieve sourced specifics on how well investor databases and tools work (Crunchbase, Signal by NFX, OpenVC, Visible, scouts, LinkedIn/X). The report writer should describe them only qualitatively: Signal by NFX is a free investor-search network, OpenVC is a free investor database and deck-submission platform, and Visible offers an investor database and CRM. These descriptions are general knowledge, not from fetched sources this session.

## 2. What investors evaluate at pre-seed/seed

### Takeaway
Team is the top selection factor in survey data. At seed, investors also screen for growth rate, retention and "why now," and a standard 10-12 slide deck (Sequoia/YC structure) is expected. Investors spend only about 2-4 minutes on a deck, so the deck has to make its case fast.

### Cited Findings
- VCs rate the management team as more important than business characteristics such as product or technology, and attribute more of a deal's success or failure to the team. They also rate deal selection as the most important of three value-creation activities, ahead of sourcing and post-investment help. — [Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/index%2Ephp/faculty-research/publications/how-venture-capitalists-make-decisions); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2801385)
- Traction benchmarks (mostly vendor-compiled; directional only):
  - SVB reported that seed companies raising in 2025 had a median revenue base of about $363K and 322% YoY growth. Cited secondhand via a blog. — [blog.mean.ceo](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=5720)
  - For sub-$1M ARR SaaS, about 100% YoY growth is "good" and about 300% is "great" (citing High Alpha). — [Causo](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/seed-mrr-benchmarks-2026)
  - Pre-seed/seed growth benchmarks: 150-300%+ YoY. Seed to Series A: 100-250% YoY. Pre-seed/seed NRR is commonly 95-100%. — [Data-Mania](https://www.data-mania.com/blog/b2b-saas-revenue-efficiency-benchmarks-2026-magic-number-rule-of-40-nrr-by-stage/)
  - Month-over-month framing: 15-25% MoM is "best-in-class" and 8-15% MoM is "solid" at pre-seed/seed. — [Stripo Research](https://research.stripo.email/ssential-saas-business-metrics-2026)
  - AI startups reportedly close seed rounds on lower revenue but face higher growth bars. One guide cites 15%+ week-over-week for AI, and 20-30% MoM with cohort retention for marketplaces and consumer. — [Causo growth guide](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/growth-rate-benchmarks-seed)
  - CRV notes investors increasingly scrutinize cohort retention and pilot-to-production conversion. AI "ARR" can include one-off or outcome-based revenue that doesn't behave like recurring revenue. — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/key-performance-indicators)
- Deck structure: the Sequoia-style deck has about 10 sections: company purpose, problem, solution, why now, market size, product, business model, competition, team, and financials. YC-style decks cover title, problem, solution, why now, market, product, traction, business model, competition, team, and ask. These outlines come from third-party template reproductions, not Sequoia or YC originals. — [Zoom/Sequoia template](https://www.zoom.com/gallery/templates/6S6b6HfCQsenuxqZLYht2Q); [Slidebean](https://slidebean.com/templates/sequoia-pitch-deck-template); [Skillselion YC deck](https://skillselion.com/skills/guia-matthieu/clawfu-skills/yc-pitch-deck)
- Deck review time (DocSend):
  - The widely cited figure is 3 min 44 s average time per deck. It comes from older DocSend research and some secondary sources misdate it. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1167693)
  - DocSend content says investors spend "less than 3 minutes" now. — [Dropbox DocSend](https://www.dropbox.com/resources/docsend-pitch-deck-research)
  - 2020 DocSend data: decks that raised got over 4 minutes of reading time, versus 1 min 30 s for decks that didn't. — [Dropbox DocSend](https://www.dropbox.com/resources/docsend-pitch-deck-research)
  - 2023 DocSend: pre-seed decks got 11% less review time year over year. — [GuruFocus/PR Newswire](https://m.gurufocus.com/news/2071592/)
- Secondary sources say the most-viewed slides are Financials, Team and Competition (unverified). — [presentations.ai](https://www.presentations.ai/blog/pitch-deck-statistics)

### Inferences
- At pre-seed, with little or no revenue, team, insight and "why now" dominate. At seed, investors now expect real usage or revenue signals and strong growth, especially for AI companies, where the bar is high-velocity growth.
- With 2-4 minutes of attention, the traction slide and team slide should come early and be scannable.

### Gaps
- I couldn't access the YC "Guide to Seed Fundraising" or the Sequoia original directly (fetch failed).
- No sourced data room checklist was found. A typical seed data room includes incorporation docs, cap table, IP assignments, financials or model, key contracts and metrics, but that is general knowledge and should be flagged as such.
- No primary 2025-2026 market-size threshold, such as a TAM floor investors require, was found.

## 3. Running the process: timeline, pipeline, momentum, lead investor, meeting counts

### Takeaway
Run a compressed, parallel process. Benchmarks suggest building a target list of about 100+ investors, expecting about 35-40% first-to-second meeting conversion, and allowing roughly 3 months. With SAFEs you can close investors one at a time as they commit, which builds momentum.

### Cited Findings
- Older DocSend/study data (about 2015; **older data**): startups contacted an average of 58 investors, held 40 meetings and took 12.5 weeks to close. There was no correlation between the number of investors contacted and the amount raised. — [PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/?p=145925)
- Metal's dataset puts first-to-second-meeting conversion at about 37% for US seed rounds, with a range of 27-55%, and recommends at least 15 meetings before benchmarking yourself. Pre-seed/seed sits at about 35%, and breakout-traction companies reach 75-85%. — [Metal data desk](https://www.metal.so/data-desk/1st-to-2nd-meeting-conversion)
- WaveUp (vendor) funnel: contact 200-300 investors, of whom 20-30% respond, narrowing to 2-9 in due diligence. It also claims at least 50 investors pitched before the first yes. — [WaveUp](https://hub.waveup.com/guides/investor-numbers)
- Slidebean suggests a shortlist of 75-150 investors and stacking intros into a 2-4 week meeting sprint (third-party advice). — [Slidebean](https://slidebean.com/blog/startups-how-to-get-seed-funding)
- YC advice for its companies: defer investor meetings until close to Demo Day, which concentrates demand. Aim to sell about 20% in the seed round (25% acceptable for a larger round), and close the first reasonable checks early rather than waiting to fill the round. — [YC Blog](https://blog.ycombinator.com/fundraising-advice-for-yc-companies)
- SAFEs let founders close investors as they agree, without waiting for the full round. YC recommends Clerky for signing them. — [Lunaray summary of YC guidance](https://lunaray.substack.com/p/fundraising-with-safes); [VCBeast](https://vcbeast.com/y-combinator-safe-agreement)
- Elad Gil's guidance for YC companies raising seed rounds is a further resource. Content was not retrieved. — [Elad Gil blog](https://blog.eladgil.com/p/for-companies-raising-seed-rounds)

### Inferences
- The playbook pattern: build a tiered list of about 100-150 investors with leads first, collect intros in a short window, run meetings in parallel over 2-4 weeks, track everything in a CRM or spreadsheet (stage, next step, date), use early commitments and SAFEs to create urgency, and secure a lead or a large anchor check to set terms.
- Because about 100 deals are considered per deal closed (Gompers), even good companies should expect a large majority of "no"s. Fewer than 40% of first meetings converting to a second is normal, not a failure.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous 2025-2026 dataset on time-to-close for seed rounds, or on how often rounds have a single lead versus being "party rounds."
- The YC guide's exact advice on meeting cadence could not be retrieved.

## 4. The 2025-2026 funding climate

### Takeaway
Venture dollars hit near-record levels in 2026, but they are extremely concentrated in AI and mega-rounds. AI took about 64% of global VC in Q3 2026, and PitchBook puts AI near 80% of trailing-12-month deal value. Deal counts, especially at seed, are falling. Non-AI founders face a tougher, bifurcated market, and the gaps between rounds are long (about 2 years).

### Cited Findings
- **Crunchbase, Q3 2026** (secondary coverage; I couldn't check the original):
  - Global venture funding was about $159B across nearly 6,000 startups, down 25% from Q2 and up 53% from a year earlier.
  - AI raised $102B, or 64% of the total.
  - A record 27 rounds of $1B or more closed, up from 16 in Q2 and 14 in Q1. Eight companies raised $3B or more, including $5B rounds for Databricks and Safe Superintelligence.
  - Year-to-date funding of about $679B is the highest first-three-quarters total on record. US companies took about $91B (57%).
  - Sources: [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-crunchbase-q3-2026-159b-venture-27-billion-dollar-rounds-ai/); [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/crunchbase-logs-27-billion-dollar-rounds-in-q3-2026-as-ai-grabs-102b-or-64-of); [ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/pulse/ai-startups-64-percent-record-q3-funding-2026)
- **KPMG Venture Pulse, Q3 2026** (press release):
  - Global VC reached US$728.3B in the first nine months of 2026.
  - Q3 regions: Americas $101.2B over 3,947 deals, of which the US was $98.4B over 3,626 deals. Asia $39.8B over 2,698 deals, with China at $27.2B. Europe $28.4B over 1,497 deals, its 8th straight quarterly increase and a 16-quarter high.
  - The release headline: "AI megadeals dominate venture capital as investors pour billions into fewer startups."
  - Sources: [KPMG Q3'26 release](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/media/press-releases/2026/10/ai-megadeals-dominate-venture-capital-as-investors-pour-billions-into-fewer-startups.html)
- **KPMG H1 2026:** US$560.4B, the highest H1 in five years. Q2 2026 was US$227.4B across 8,440 deals. — [KPMG H1 release](https://kpmg.com/ua/en/media/press-releases/2026/08/global-vc-investment-reaches-us-dollar-560-4-billion-at-mid-year.html); [KPMG July release](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/media/press-releases/2026/07/vc-investment-already-at-five-year-high-of-billions.html)
- **Conflict:** Crunchbase's Q3 total (about $159B) and the Q3 implied by KPMG (about $168B) differ because of methodology. Crunchbase's year-to-date figure ($679B) is also lower than KPMG's ($728B). Cite each with its source.
- **PitchBook, 2026:** AI accounts for close to 80% of trailing-12-month venture deal value but only about 43% of deal count, a "bifurcated dealmaking environment." Global liquidity "remains a challenge." — [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/newsletter/scribe-secures-30m) (snippet surfaced on a PitchBook listing page; low-confidence URL attribution)
- **PitchBook-NVCA, Q3 2025** (**older**): AI was 64% of deal value (a record) and a record 39.5% of deal count. AI VC deal value was $54.8B over 1,086 deals in Q3 2025, versus a record $72.4B in Q1 2025. — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/10/03/ai-drives-record-share-venture-deal-value-third-quarter-fundraising-slump-continues/); [PitchBook report page](https://www.pitchbook.com/news/reports/q3-2025-pitchbook-nvca-venture-monitor); [PitchBook AI VC Trends Preview](https://files.pitchbook.com/website/files/pdf/Q3_2025_AI_VC_Trends_Preview.pdf)
- **Seed sizes and valuations (Carta):**
  - Q1 2025 (**older**): median seed pre-money was about $16M, up about 18% YoY. Only 401 new seed rounds closed (down 28% YoY), totalling $1.2B (down 37%). — [Carta Q1 2025](https://carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q1-2025/)
  - Pre-seed, Q2 2026: US startups raised $3.19B across more than 11,500 SAFEs and notes. In Q2 2025 it was $3.22B across 14,825 instruments, so the count fell while the average instrument rose 27% YoY to $276K. — [Carta State of Pre-Seed Q2 2026](https://carta.com/data/state-of-pre-seed-q2-2026/)
  - Top of market: the 95th-percentile seed valuation hit $200M in Q2 2026, up from $72.2M a year earlier. — [Carta](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-seed-valuations-95th-percentile-2026/)
  - Secondary claims about Carta data (unverified): median priced software seed of $4.1M raised (6 months to July 2026), a median seed valuation of about $24.3M, and a median seed post-money of $21.6M in Q1 2026. — [WaveUp](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/); [Flowjam](https://flowjam.com/blog/seed-round-valuation-2025-complete-founders-guide)
- **Time between rounds (Carta):**
  - Median of 696 days between rounds across all stages in Q2 2025. Series B took a median 2.8 years after Series A in Q1 2025, the longest on record. — [Carta Q2 2025](https://carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q2-2025/); [Carta Q1 2025](https://carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q1-2025/)
  - Seed to Series A: 712 days in Q2 2024 (**older**). — [Carta Q2 2024](https://carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q2-2024/)
- **Non-AI founders:**
  - Startups with AI features raised 83% more than others in 2025, and AI took about 50% ($202B) of tech funding in 2025. — [HackerNoon](https://sia.hackernoon.com/venture-capital-tilts-toward-ai-as-non-ai-startups-face-funding-squeeze)
  - Many funds have realigned their mandates to AI, and non-AI founders often face "how does AI fit in?" as the first question (vendor blog, April 2026). — [Qubit Capital](https://qubit.capital/blog/fundraising-for-non-ai-startups)
  - Only 9% of Series A companies raised a Series B within two years, versus 25% previously (Carta via TechCrunch, Dec 2024; **older**). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/17/ai-boom-masks-fundraising-struggles-for-non-ai-startups)
  - AI seed rounds are claimed to carry a 42% valuation premium over non-AI peers (unverified vendor blog). — [ai2.work](https://ai2.work/blog/why-seed-stage-ai-valuations-are-at-all-time-highs)
  - "Seedstrapping" (raise once, then reach revenue with a small team) is emerging as an alternative path. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2932811)

### Inferences
- The headline "record VC year" mostly reflects a few dozen AI mega-rounds. A median founder, especially non-AI, faces fewer seed deals, a higher bar and about 2-year gaps between rounds. Plan for 24+ months of runway per round.
- Valuations are bimodal: the top AI seeds reach $100M-200M post-money, while the median stays in the high teens to low $20Ms.

### Gaps
- I couldn't retrieve the full Q3 2026 PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor numbers (US deal counts, seed medians).
- No primary 2026 dataset isolating non-AI seed round sizes or valuations was found.
- Investor sentiment surveys for 2026 (for example, LP liquidity or fund-manager outlook) weren't retrieved beyond PitchBook's "liquidity remains a challenge" note.
- Regional seed medians outside the US (Europe, India, LatAm, Africa) weren't found.

## 5. Red flags and scams

### Takeaway
Legitimate investors never charge founders money to invest. Upfront "due diligence," "compliance," "legal" or "escrow" fees are hallmarks of advance-fee fraud. Paid "pitch access" syndicates charging thousands are, at best, low-value and are widely criticised.

### Cited Findings
- The SEC investor alert describes advance-fee fraud: fees labelled as deposits, processing charges or regulatory fees, often routed to "escrow agents or lawyers" to look legitimate. — [Investor.gov (SEC)](https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-alert-be-lookout-advance-fee-fraud)
- Common scam pattern: an "investor" offers a large check, then asks for a small upfront compliance or legal fee. — [The VC Corner](https://www.thevccorner.com/p/startup-fundraising-scams); [Rare Founders](https://rarefounders-newsletter.beehiiv.com/p/10-fundraising-scams-every-startup-founder-should-know-2025)
- Pay-to-pitch: self-styled "angel syndicates" charge $5,000-$20,000 to pitch to investors, sometimes framed as "due diligence" support, and access often never materializes. — [Great British Business Show](https://www.greatbritishbusinessshow.co.uk/blog-library/10-fundraising-scams-entrepreneur-know-2025); [Rare Founders](https://rarefounders-newsletter.beehiiv.com/p/10-fundraising-scams-every-startup-founder-should-know-2025)
- Data harvesting: fake "analysts" request the financial model, customer list or cap table, then vanish. — [Crunchbase blog](https://about.crunchbase.com/blog/startup-scams-and-scam-adjacent-activity)
- Debate over pitch fees: Jason Calacanis called angel-group pitch fees "payola," while others defend small fees as cost recovery and filtering. — [Gust](https://gust.com/blog/pitching-is-never-free/); [Caycon](https://www.caycon.com/blog/presenting-your-case-to-investors-is-rarely-free)
- Due diligence on investors: check their deal history on Crunchbase or AngelList, ask portfolio founders for references, and check email domains and WHOIS records. — [The VC Corner](https://www.thevccorner.com/p/startup-fundraising-scams); [Crunchbase blog](https://about.crunchbase.com/blog/startup-scams-and-scam-adjacent-activity)
- Advance-fee scams are also documented by other regulators, for example the Securities Commission of The Bahamas. — [SCB](https://www.scb.gov.bs/?p=2309); [Univ. of Miami Investor Rights Clinic](https://investorrights.law.miami.edu/protecting-against-advance-fee-scams/)

### Inferences
- Simple rule: money flows from investor to company, never the reverse. Back-channel references from portfolio founders are the strongest check.

### Gaps
- Predatory terms (full-ratchet anti-dilution, participating preferred with multiples above 1x, heavy liquidation preferences, excessive control or redemption rights, uncapped or very low-cap SAFE stacking) weren't covered by any source retrieved this session. The report writer should flag that section as unsourced or general knowledge, or omit it.
- No 2025-2026 data on the prevalence or losses of founder-targeted scams was found.

## 6. Founder dilution benchmarks per round

### Takeaway
A typical round now costs about 16-20% dilution; Carta's 2025 median across seed to Series C fell to about 16%. Median founding-team ownership is about 55% after seed, about 37% after Series A and about 23% after Series B.

### Cited Findings
- Carta 2025 in review: median dilution across seed to Series C fell from about 18% to 16%. Series B saw the biggest drop, from about 15% to 12.9%. — [Carta 2025 in review](https://carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q4-2025-full-report/)
- Median Series A dilution was 17.9% in Q1 2025, down from 20.9% a year earlier. — [Carta Q1 2025](https://carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q1-2025/)
- Median founding-team ownership (Carta): 55.1% after seed, 36.6% after Series A, 23.5% after Series B, 17.5% after Series C. Only about 1 in 5 founding teams still owns 50% or more after Series A. — [Carta LinkedIn data post](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-founders-rarely-majority-after-series-a/); [Carta Founder Ownership 2026](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership-2026/)
- Median ownership after Series A by sector: 37.2% for digital companies versus 30.3% for physical ones. — [Carta Founder Ownership 2026 full report](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership-2026-full-report/) (via secondary summary)
- Secondary per-round medians (Carta-based, unverified): priced seed about 18-19.5%, Series A about 18%, Series B about 14%, Series C about 10%. — [WaveUp](https://waveup.com/blog/startup-dilution-per-round/); [Causo](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/founder-dilution-benchmarks-across-rounds-2026)
- Option-pool top-ups and SAFE conversions landing in the priced round often make real dilution 5-10 points worse than the headline figure. — [Causo](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/founder-dilution-benchmarks-across-rounds-2026)
- YC norm: target about 20% sold at seed, with 25% acceptable for a larger round. — [YC Blog](https://blog.ycombinator.com/fundraising-advice-for-yc-companies)

### Inferences
- Model the cap table including the pre-money option pool and all outstanding SAFEs. Stacking several post-money SAFEs before a priced seed can quietly push total seed-stage dilution above 25%.

### Gaps
- Pre-seed dilution medians (SAFE-only rounds) weren't found. Non-US dilution norms weren't found.
